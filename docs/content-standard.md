# Content standard

One structure, one page template, one component vocabulary and one assessment format for every course. Updated 2026-10-16: the Exam section is removed, Review gains a Decoding section, quizzes let the learner choose questions, and learning progress is tracked in the browser (sections 2.4 to 2.7). The structure follows Google's developer documentation (top bar with section tabs, sidebar, "On this page", topic tabs, pager); the visual theme stays the site's paper-and-ink look (cream and navy, Inter and Fraunces, 2px ink frames, hard offset shadows). This extends the chapter shape in [content-architecture.md](content-architecture.md); where the two disagree, this document wins.

## 1. Diagnosis: why the notes feel like components smashed together

Measured on 2026-10-15 across `content/docs`.

### Five separate note systems

| Courses | How notes are produced | What it looks like |
|---|---|---|
| CSI131, CSI141, CSI161, MAT111, COM141, CSI132, CSI142, MAT122, STA122, COM142 | Generated by `TopicNotesFlow`, `ChapterReview`, `ChapterQuestions`, `ChapterExam` from TypeScript data | Page title is just "Notes"; the description is repeated under it; a row of Orient/Understand/Apply/Verify/Transfer chips; a checklist at the top; ten "worked cases" |

## 2. The standard

### 2.1 Course and topic shape (the sidebar)

```text
semN/<course>/
  index.mdx               Overview: what the course covers, topic cards, how it is assessed
  01-<topic>/             Numbered folder; meta.json title "Topic name" (the sidebar adds "1.")
    index.mdx             Overview: what you'll learn, prerequisites, the pages below, an optional visual study map
    notes.mdx | notes/    Notes: one page, or a folder of lessons for a large topic
    cheat-sheet.mdx       Cheat sheet (optional)
    review.mdx            Review: flashcards, a quiz you can tailor, and decoding the questions
    questions.mdx         Practice: guided questions with worked solutions
    resources.mdx         Resources for this topic (optional)
  resources.mdx           Resources for the whole course
```

The sidebar and the topic tabs use the same labels everywhere, in this order: Overview, Notes, Cheat sheet, Review, Practice, Resources. Overview, Notes, Review and Practice are required; Cheat sheet and Resources are optional. A topic may leave out an optional page; it may not invent another one. A course folder holds only its overview, its numbered topics and its resources page; course-level extras such as a playground or a decoding guide move to `/docs/playgrounds` and into Review.

There is no Exam page. The site has no exam papers to build one from, and a mock exam written without a paper would be invented. When a course does have a past paper, it is used as context for review questions and listed in Resources (section 2.4). `exam.mdx` and `exam-practice.mdx` redirect to Review.

### 2.1.1 Sidebar labels

One rule for each level of the sidebar. Code applies it (`lib/topic-pages.ts`, used by the page tree in `lib/source.ts`), so the sidebar, the breadcrumb, the topic tabs and the pager always read the same, and no course can drift.

| Level | Label | Comes from | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| Course | `CODE: Title` | the course `meta.json` title | `CSI243: Functional Programming` |
| Course pages | `Course Overview`, `Course Resources` | fixed | |
| Topic | `N. Title` | N from the folder (`06-` is 6, never `06.`), Title from the topic `meta.json` | `6. Lists and Tuples` |
| Topic pages | `Overview`, `Notes`, `Cheat sheet`, `Review`, `Practice`, `Resources` | fixed (section 2.1) | |
| Lesson | `N.M Title` | N is the topic number, M the lesson's place in `notes/meta.json`, Title the page title | `6.4 List Comprehensions` |

Rules that follow:

- **A title carries no number.** Write `title: "List Comprehensions"`, never `"4. List Comprehensions"` or `"6.4 List Comprehensions"`. The number is computed from the folder name and the order in `meta.json`, so reordering or inserting a lesson renumbers everything and nothing is typed twice.
- **A topic is always a numbered folder** (`NN-slug`), so every topic shows a number. A topic with a single `notes.mdx` and a topic with a `notes/` folder both show one entry called Notes; the lessons sit beneath it.
- **A lesson title names the lesson**, in a few words that say what it teaches. "Notes", "Lesson 1" and "Introduction" do not.
- **No status markers** in a title, such as `(WIP)` or `(draft)`. A page that is unfinished says so in a Callout.
- Course titles are `CODE: Title` with a colon and one space, the code in capitals.

`npm run audit:content-standard` reports `label-typed-number`, `label-status` and `label-course-title`.

### 2.1.2 Topic Overview layout

Every topic's `index.mdx` (the Overview tab) has the same sections in the same order, so a reader always finds what a topic is for, what it assumes, and where its pages are:

1. **Title and description.** The title is the topic's name without a number, such as "Lists, Tuples and Comprehensions" (never "Topic 6 overview" or "Chapter Overview"); the description is one sentence.
2. **An opening paragraph** of two or three sentences: what the topic is about and why it matters.
3. **`<LearningObjectives items={[...]} />`** (it draws its own "Learning objectives" heading): three to six outcomes, each starting with a verb (Explain, Write, Trace, Compare, Prove), the same outcomes the notes teach and Practice tests.
4. **`<Prerequisites items={[...]} />`** (it draws its own "Prerequisites" heading): the earlier topics or skills this one assumes, named in plain text (the items are strings, not links), or "Nothing beyond the start of the course" for a first topic.
5. **`## Visual study map`** (optional): the topic's canvas or diagram, when it has one.
6. **`## Pages in this topic`**: a table with one row for each lesson in Notes (in order, with the question it answers) and one for each of Cheat sheet, Review, Practice and Resources that exists.

An Overview holds no lesson content, quiz or exercise. A longer introduction from an older page moves into the first lesson. The audit reports `overview-title` and `overview-layout`.

### 2.2 Notes page template

Every notes page, in this order:

1. **Title** is the lesson name (never just "Notes"). **Description** is one sentence. Nothing repeats them.
2. **What you'll learn**: three to five objectives (`LearningObjectives`).
3. **Sections**: each `##` section teaches one idea in this order: plain explanation → definition (`KeyTerm`) → why it works → example (code with output, figure or table) → at most one callout.
4. **Common mistakes**: one section of `mistake` callouts, or a table.
5. **Summary**: one `key` callout with the points to remember.
6. **Check your understanding**: a `## Check your understanding` heading, then `<CheckQuestions>` holding three to five `<CheckQuestion question="…">answer</CheckQuestion>` items, at the end of the page, not between sections.
7. Pager (automatic).

### 2.3 Component vocabulary

Notes use only these. Everything else is either retired or rendered through one of them.

| Element | Use | Replaces |
|---|---|---|
| Prose, `##`, `###`, lists | Teaching | |
| Code block with `title="File.java"`, then an `Output` block | Programs and their output | separate unlabelled input and output blocks |
| Figure with caption (`SiteDiagram`, image, `Mermaid`) | Diagrams | |
| Table | Comparisons, traces | `ComparisonTable` (renders as a table) |
| `Callout` with `note`, `tip`, `caution`, `mistake`, `key`, `analogy` | Asides, one meaning each | `PresetCard`, `HighlightCard`, `CommonMistake`, `RecognitionStrategy` |
| `KeyTerm` | A definition or theorem | `Card`, `ConceptBlock`, `TheoremBlock`, MAT221 `Concept` and `Theorem` |
| `Steps` | A procedure or a worked example | `StepByStep`, `MethodSteps`, `InteractiveStepper`, `FumaSteps` |
| `LearningObjectives`, `Prerequisites` | Page opening | the generated chip row and top checklist |
| Interactive pieces (`JavaTryIt`, `HaskellTryIt`, algorithm stories) | Running or watching code | one shared frame: title bar, stage, controls |

Retired components keep working: they render through the standard element, so old pages look consistent before their content is migrated.

**Visual study maps.** A topic with an Excalidraw canvas (`public/diagrams/<semester>/<course>/canvases/`) embeds it on its Overview page, under a `## Visual study map` heading below the opening paragraph, as `ExcalidrawCanvas` with `src`, `title` and `expectedMB`. Notes pages do not embed canvases. At 3 MB or more the canvas loads when the reader clicks, so a large file is never downloaded by scrolling past it. The audit fails when a canvas points at a file that does not exist.

### 2.4 Assessment

| Kind | Where | Component | Data |
|---|---|---|---|
| Check your understanding | End of each notes page | `CheckQuestions` (expand to reveal, not scored) | in the MDX, three to five items |
| Review: recall | `review.mdx` | `FlashcardDeck`, then `QuizRef` | `content/flashcard-data`, `content/quiz-data` |
| Review: decoding | `review.mdx`, a `## Decoding the questions` section | `CommandWords`, `DecodeQuestion` | `content/decoding/*.json` |
| Practice | `questions.mdx` | `ExamQuestion` / `SubQuestion` with worked solutions | in the MDX |

Generated courses (Semesters I and II) render through the same components.

**Quizzes you can tailor.** A quiz is one question at a time with a Check button, and a score at the end with a bar for each topic and a button to retry the missed questions. A quiz whose questions fall into more than one group first lets the learner pick the topics to practise and, for a long quiz, how many questions. A quiz with one group starts straight away. The chooser is deliberately small: topics and a count, nothing else. Quiz data stays in JSON, and the extra fields are optional so every existing quiz keeps working:

| Field | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `group` | The topic or section the question belongs to (a chip in the chooser, a bar in the results) |
| `level` | `foundation`, `developing` or `exam`. A tag in the data only; the quiz does not show or filter by it |
| `steps` | A short worked solution shown after answering |
| `source` | Where the question comes from, for example a past paper and question number |
| `id` | A stable question id; if missing, one is derived from the question text |

**Decoding the questions.** The `## Decoding the questions` section of a review page teaches how to read a question before answering it. It has the three-step method, a collapsible table of command words (define, explain, compare, calculate and so on) that says what each one asks for, and worked decodings of real questions from that topic: the question as written, the plain-English rewrite, and how to answer. The generic command words are shared by every course. A course can add its own dictionary, as CSI243 does for Haskell, in `content/decoding/<course>.json`.

**Past papers.** When a course has a past paper, add it to the course's Resources page and use it when writing review questions: set `source` on each question it inspired. No page presents a paper as a mock exam.

### 2.5 Enforcement

`npm run audit:content-standard` reports every course against this standard (`--verbose` lists each finding, `--course <code>` narrows it to one course, `--json` is for tools, `--strict` exits 1 on any finding). It flags:

- a topic folder contains a page outside the list in 2.1, or lists them out of order; an `exam` or `exam-practice` page is an error;
- a notes page has no description, repeats its title as a heading, or uses a retired component;
- a notes page has quiz components before its last section;
- a review, practice or exam page uses a component other than the one in 2.4;
- a page built by a generator or by the MAT221 source site (counted separately, fixed in phase 4).

Baseline on 2026-10-15: 0 of 16 courses met the standard, 1,184 findings. After the Exam section was removed on 2026-10-16: 1 of 16 (MGT202), 1,030 findings. It joins `npm run audit:content` with `--strict` once every course passes.

### 2.6 Progress, streaks and rewards

Learning progress is tracked in the reader's browser, with no account and no backend, because the content is free. The design is in [plans/progress-system.md](plans/progress-system.md). In short:

- A streak counts the days on which the reader did something that counts: read a page, answered a quiz question, flipped a flashcard, opened a check question.
- Experience points and badges reward the same actions. The rules live in `content/progress/rewards.json`, so a badge or a level can be added without changing code.
- A dashboard at `/docs/dashboard` has five sections: an overview (a greeting, today's goal, what to read next), the courses the reader chose, trend graphs, badges (each with its own artwork) and settings. A panel at the top of the sidebar shows the streak and today's goal.
- The site encourages without nagging: messages fit the reader's situation and are data in `content/progress/messages.json`.
- State is stored in `localStorage` with a small summary in a first-party cookie. It is versioned and migrated, so a change to the format or to the content never loses a reader's history. The reader can export and import it, switch tracking off, or reset it by typing RESET to confirm.

### 2.7 Designing for constant updates

Content changes every week, so everything that records history follows these rules:

- Identifiers are stable. A page is keyed by its path, a quiz question by its `id` (or a hash of its text), a badge by its `id`. Renamed pages are listed in `content/progress/aliases.json` so earlier progress follows them.
- Storage is versioned. Each change to the stored shape adds a migration; nothing reads a field that an older version might lack without a default.
- Rules are data. XP values, levels and badges are JSON, with a version number, and unknown rule types are ignored rather than failing.
- Removing content never removes earned rewards.

## 3. Migration plan

| Phase | Work | Who |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Site chrome, tokens, navigation, Overview, timer fixes | Done on `feat/ui-material-theme`, not yet committed |
| 2 | Agree this document; write the audit script; report conformance per course | Opus |
| 3 | Render every overlapping component through the standard elements (no content changes) | Opus |
| 4 | Generators: Semesters I and II, CSI247 study guides, MAT221 | Opus, one at a time, verified in the browser |
| 5 | Hand-written content: CSI243, CSI213, CSI323, the rest of CSI247 | Sonnet agents in parallel, one course each, held to the audit; Opus reviews every diff |

Phase 5 is the only phase that suits cheaper parallel agents: the rules are fixed by then and the audit catches drift. Before phases 2 and 3 exist, each agent would invent its own standard, which is how the site got here.

## 4. Decisions

Agreed on 2026-10-15:

1. **In-note checks.** Quizzes inside notes move to one "Check your understanding" block at the end of each notes page, with answers revealed on demand.
2. **MAT221.** Rebuilt on the standard template, sidebar shape and assessment components; the embedded source site is retired once its content has moved.
3. **CSI247 and CSI243.** The ownership notes in `WORK_ORDERS.md` are out of date; this work may change both courses.
4. **Renamed pages.** Every renamed or moved page gets a permanent redirect from its old URL.

Added on 2026-10-16:

5. **No Exam section.** There are no exam papers to build one from, so Exam pages are removed from every course (75 pages) and redirect to Review. This also removes the question of writing missing exam pages for CSI243, CSI247, CSI323 and CSI213.
6. **Past papers.** When a paper exists, it is added to Resources and used as context for review questions through each question's `source` field.
7. **Decoding.** Decoding exam and lab language becomes a section of every review page, not a course-level page. CSI243's `decoding-questions` page is folded into its reviews.
8. **Quizzes.** Quizzes let the learner pick topics and how many questions, kept to those two choices so the quiz stays simple.
9. **Progress.** Streaks, rewards and a dashboard are tracked in the browser, designed for constant updates and backward compatibility.
