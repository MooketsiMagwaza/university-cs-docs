Ten questions, grouped by what they test, each with its marks. Work a question on paper with a trace table before opening the solution. The lessons that teach each form are [Recursion by Example](/docs/sem3/csi247/02-recursion/notes/recursion-by-example), [Recursion on Arrays and Strings by Example](/docs/sem3/csi247/02-recursion/notes/recursion-on-data-by-example), [Recursion Patterns by Example](/docs/sem3/csi247/02-recursion/notes/recursion-patterns-by-example) and [Recursion Exam Practice](/docs/sem3/csi247/02-recursion/notes/recursion-exam-practice), which has sixteen more.

**Marking guide.** A trace receives full credit only when the calls going down and the returns coming up are shown separately. Complexity must include the call-stack space. A base case alone is insufficient: the recursive case must make measurable progress towards it, and a method that calls itself with a step of more than one needs a base case that cannot be stepped over.
