## 5.2 Poisson Distribution

The Poisson distribution is used to model the number of times a rare event occurs over a fixed interval of time, space, or volume, given a known average rate of occurrence.

<Card>

### Definition 5.2.1 (Poisson Distribution)

A random variable $X$ follows a **Poisson Distribution** with parameter $\lambda > 0$ (the average rate of occurrence), denoted $X \sim \text{Poi}(\lambda)$, if its PMF is:
$$
p(x) = \frac{e^{-\lambda} \lambda^x}{x!} \quad \text{for } x = 0, 1, 2, \dots
$$

</Card>

<StepByStep>
  <StepByStepTitle>Proof: Expectation of the Poisson Distribution ($E[X] = \lambda$)</StepByStepTitle>

  <Step number={1}>
    <StepTitle>Apply the Definition of Expectation</StepTitle>

    $E[X] = \sum_{x=0}^\infty x \cdot p(x) = \sum_{x=0}^\infty x \frac{e^{-\lambda} \lambda^x}{x!}$
  </Step>

  <Step number={2}>
    <StepTitle>Drop the Zero Term and Factor</StepTitle>

    The $x=0$ term is $0$. For $x \ge 1$, we can cancel $x$ with $x!$ to leave $(x-1)!$ in the denominator:
    $E[X] = \sum_{x=1}^\infty \frac{e^{-\lambda} \lambda^x}{(x-1)!}$
    Factor out one $\lambda$ and the constant $e^{-\lambda}$:
    $E[X] = \lambda e^{-\lambda} \sum_{x=1}^\infty \frac{\lambda^{x-1}}{(x-1)!}$
  </Step>

  <Step number={3}>
    <StepTitle>Apply Index Shifting</StepTitle>

    Let $y = x - 1$. As $x$ goes from $1$ to $\infty$, $y$ goes from $0$ to $\infty$:
    $E[X] = \lambda e^{-\lambda} \sum_{y=0}^\infty \frac{\lambda^y}{y!}$
  </Step>

  <Step number={4}>
    <StepTitle>Recognize the Maclaurin Series for $e^\lambda$</StepTitle>

    From Theorem 1.4.7, $\sum_{y=0}^\infty \frac{\lambda^y}{y!} = e^\lambda$.
    Substituting this yields:
    $E[X] = \lambda e^{-\lambda} (e^\lambda) = \lambda e^0 = \lambda$. $\blacksquare$
  </Step>
</StepByStep>

<StepByStep>
  <StepByStepTitle>Proof: Variance of the Poisson Distribution ($V[X] = \lambda$)</StepByStepTitle>

  <Step number={1}>
    <StepTitle>Strategy: Use Factorial Moments</StepTitle>

    Directly calculating $E[X^2]$ is cumbersome. Instead, we calculate the second factorial moment $E[X(X-1)]$ and use the identity:
    $V[X] = E[X(X-1)] + E[X] - (E[X])^2$
  </Step>

  <Step number={2}>
    <StepTitle>Calculate $E[X(X-1)]$</StepTitle>

    $E[X(X-1)] = \sum_{x=0}^\infty x(x-1) \frac{e^{-\lambda} \lambda^x}{x!}$
    The terms for $x=0$ and $x=1$ are zero. For $x \ge 2$, cancel $x(x-1)$ with $x!$ to leave $(x-2)!$:
    $E[X(X-1)] = \sum_{x=2}^\infty \frac{e^{-\lambda} \lambda^x}{(x-2)!}$
  </Step>

  <Step number={3}>
    <StepTitle>Factor and Shift Index</StepTitle>

    Factor out $\lambda^2 e^{-\lambda}$:
    $E[X(X-1)] = \lambda^2 e^{-\lambda} \sum_{x=2}^\infty \frac{\lambda^{x-2}}{(x-2)!}$
    Let $y = x - 2$. The sum becomes $\sum_{y=0}^\infty \frac{\lambda^y}{y!} = e^\lambda$.
    Thus, $E[X(X-1)] = \lambda^2 e^{-\lambda} (e^\lambda) = \lambda^2$.
  </Step>

  <Step number={4}>
    <StepTitle>Apply the Variance Identity</StepTitle>

    $V[X] = \lambda^2 + \lambda - (\lambda)^2 = \lambda$. $\blacksquare$
  </Step>
</StepByStep>
