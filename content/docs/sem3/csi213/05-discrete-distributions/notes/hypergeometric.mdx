## 5.4 Hypergeometric Distribution

While the Binomial distribution assumes independent trials (sampling *with* replacement), real-world sampling is often done *without* replacement from a finite population. This changes the probability of success on each subsequent draw, requiring the Hypergeometric distribution.

<Card>

### Definition 5.4.1 (Hypergeometric Distribution)

Consider a finite population of size $N$, containing exactly $K$ "successes" and $N-K$ "failures". If we draw a sample of size $n$ **without replacement**, the random variable $X$ representing the number of successes in the sample follows a **Hypergeometric Distribution**, denoted $X \sim \text{Hypergeometric}(N, K, n)$.

**Probability Mass Function (PMF):**
$$
p(x) = \frac{\binom{K}{x} \binom{N-K}{n-x}}{\binom{N}{n}}
$$
for $\max(0, n - (N - K)) \le x \le \min(n, K)$.

</Card>

<StepByStep>
  <StepByStepTitle>Combinatorial Intuition: Deriving the Hypergeometric PMF</StepByStepTitle>

  <Step number={1}>
    <StepTitle>Define the Sample Space</StepTitle>

    The total number of ways to choose $n$ items from a population of $N$ is $\binom{N}{n}$. This is our denominator (equally likely outcomes).
  </Step>

  <Step number={2}>
    <StepTitle>Count Favorable Outcomes</StepTitle>

    To get exactly $x$ successes, we must choose $x$ items from the $K$ available successes: $\binom{K}{x}$ ways.
    Simultaneously, we must choose the remaining $n-x$ items from the $N-K$ available failures: $\binom{N-K}{n-x}$ ways.
  </Step>

  <Step number={3}>
    <StepTitle>Apply the Product Rule</StepTitle>

    By the Rule of Product, the total number of favorable samples is $\binom{K}{x} \binom{N-K}{n-x}$.
    Dividing by the total sample space yields the PMF. $\blacksquare$
  </Step>
</StepByStep>

<HighlightCard
  variant="neutral"
  title="Theorem 5.4.1: Moments and the Finite Population Correction (FPC)"
  description="The expectation matches the Binomial intuition, but the variance is reduced by the FPC factor due to sampling without replacement."
>

For $X \sim \text{Hypergeometric}(N, K, n)$, let $p = \frac{K}{N}$ (the proportion of successes in the population).

**Expectation:**
$$
E[X] = np
$$

**Variance:**
$$
V[X] = np(1-p) \left( \frac{N-n}{N-1} \right)
$$
The term $\left( \frac{N-n}{N-1} \right)$ is the **Finite Population Correction (FPC)** factor.

</HighlightCard>

<Card>

### Definition 5.4.2 (Intuition Behind the FPC)

Sampling without replacement provides more information about the remaining population, thereby reducing uncertainty (variance) compared to sampling with replacement (Binomial). 

- If $n = N$ (we sample the entire population), the FPC is $0$, and $V[X] = 0$ (no uncertainty, we know exactly how many successes we have).
- As the population size $N \to \infty$ relative to the sample size $n$, the FPC approaches $1$. In this limit, the Hypergeometric distribution converges to the Binomial distribution, as sampling without replacement becomes practically indistinguishable from sampling with replacement.

</Card>

<StepByStep>
  <StepByStepTitle>Example: Hypergeometric Probability and Variance</StepByStepTitle>

  <Step number={1}>
    <StepTitle>Problem Statement</StepTitle>

    A batch of 20 microchips contains 5 defective chips. A quality inspector randomly selects 4 chips without replacement. Let $X$ be the number of defective chips found. Find $P(X = 1)$ and $V[X]$.
  </Step>

  <Step number={2}>
    <StepTitle>Identify Parameters</StepTitle>

    $N = 20$ (total population), $K = 5$ (total defects), $n = 4$ (sample size).
    We want $x = 1$.
  </Step>

  <Step number={3}>
    <StepTitle>Calculate Probability $P(X = 1)$</StepTitle>

    $P(X = 1) = \frac{\binom{5}{1} \binom{15}{3}}{\binom{20}{4}} = \frac{5 \times 455}{4845} = \frac{2275}{4845} \approx 0.4696$
  </Step>

  <Step number={4}>
    <StepTitle>Calculate Variance with FPC</StepTitle>

    Proportion of defects: $p = \frac{K}{N} = \frac{5}{20} = 0.25$.
    Binomial variance component: $np(1-p) = 4(0.25)(0.75) = 0.75$.
    FPC factor: $\frac{N-n}{N-1} = \frac{20-4}{20-1} = \frac{16}{19} \approx 0.8421$.
    $V[X] = 0.75 \times \frac{16}{19} = \frac{12}{19} \approx 0.6316$.
  </Step>
</StepByStep>
