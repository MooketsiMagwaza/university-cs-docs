## Bernoulli and choosing the model

<ExamQuestion title="Foundation: One component">

A component passes inspection with probability $0.92$. Let $X=1$ if it passes and $X=0$ if it fails. State the distribution of $X$, write its PMF, and find its mean and variance.

<details>
<summary>Show the complete worked solution</summary>

<Steps>
  <Step title="Name the model">
    One success/failure trial means $X\sim\operatorname{Bernoulli}(0.92)$.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Write both masses">
    $P(X=1)=0.92$ and $P(X=0)=0.08$.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Mean">
    $E[X]=p=0.92$.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Variance">
    $\operatorname{Var}(X)=0.92(0.08)=0.0736$.
  </Step>
</Steps>

**Final answer.** $\boxed{X\sim\operatorname{Bernoulli}(0.92), E[X]=0.92, \operatorname{Var}(X)=0.0736}$.

</details>

</ExamQuestion>

<ExamQuestion title="Developing: Same experiment, different variable">

A basketball player scores each free throw independently with probability $0.7$. Classify each variable: (i) the number scored in 12 attempts, (ii) the attempt number of the first score, and (iii) the attempt number of the fourth score.

<details>
<summary>Show the complete worked solution</summary>

<Steps>
  <Step title="Question (i)">
    The 12 attempts are fixed and successes are counted: binomial.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Question (ii)">
    One success is fixed and trials are counted: geometric.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Question (iii)">
    Four successes are fixed and trials are counted: negative binomial.
  </Step>
  <Step title="See the single rule">
    The physical experiment is unchanged. Only the definition of the random variable changes.
  </Step>
</Steps>

**Final answer.** $\boxed{\text{Binomial; Geometric; Negative binomial}}$.

</details>

</ExamQuestion>

<ExamQuestion title="Exam: Use indicator variables">

A system contains 6 independent sensors. Sensor $i$ activates with probability $p_i$. Define $I_i=1$ if sensor $i$ activates and $0$ otherwise, and let $T=\sum_{i=1}^{6} I_i$. Without assuming the probabilities are equal, express $E[T]$.

<details>
<summary>Show the complete worked solution</summary>

<Steps>
  <Step title="Recognise each indicator">
    $I_i$ is Bernoulli with $E[I_i]=p_i$.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Write the count as a sum">
    $T=\sum_{i=1}^{6} I_i$ counts the activated sensors.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Use linearity">
    $E[T]=\sum_{i=1}^{6}E[I_i]$. Independence is not required for linearity of expectation.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Substitute the means">
    $E[T]=p_1+\cdots+p_6$.
  </Step>
</Steps>

**Final answer.** $\boxed{E[T]=\sum_{i=1}^{6}p_i}$.

</details>

</ExamQuestion>

## Binomial

<ExamQuestion title="Foundation: Exactly three correct">

A student guesses independently on a 5-question true-or-false quiz. Let $X$ be the number of correct answers. Find the probability that the student answers exactly 3 questions correctly.

<details>
<summary>Show the complete worked solution</summary>

<Steps>
  <Step title="Verify the model">
    There are $n=5$ fixed independent trials and $p=0.5$ on each trial, so $X\sim\operatorname{Bin}(5,0.5)$.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Translate the wording">
    “Exactly 3” means $P(X=3)$.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Substitute">
    $P(X=3)=\binom53(0.5)^3(0.5)^2$.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Evaluate">
    $10(0.5)^5=\frac{10}{32}=0.3125$.
  </Step>
</Steps>

**Final answer.** $\boxed{P(X=3)=0.3125}$.

</details>

</ExamQuestion>

<ExamQuestion title="Developing: At least six successful logins">

A login attempt succeeds independently with probability $0.7$. Eight attempts are made. Find the expected number of successes and the probability that at least 6 attempts succeed.

<details>
<summary>Show the complete worked solution</summary>

<Steps>
  <Step title="Model and mean">
    $X\sim\operatorname{Bin}(8,0.7)$, so $E[X]=8(0.7)=5.6$.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Translate the tail">
    $P(X\ge 6)=P(X=6)+P(X=7)+P(X=8)$.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Write the sum">
    $\sum_{k=6}^{8}\binom8k(0.7)^k(0.3)^{8-k}$.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Evaluate">
    The three terms total approximately $0.5518$.
  </Step>
</Steps>

**Final answer.** $\boxed{E[X]=5.6,\quad P(X\ge 6)\approx0.5518}$.

</details>

</ExamQuestion>

<ExamQuestion title="Exam: Infer the parameter and solve a tail">

For a binomial variable $X$, the number of trials is $n=20$ and the expected number of successes is $E[X]=5$. Determine $p$. Then calculate $P(X=0)$ and $P(X\ge 1)$, explaining why the second calculation should use a complement.

<details>
<summary>Show the complete worked solution</summary>

<Steps>
  <Step title="Recover p">
    $E[X]=np$, so $5=20p$ and $p=0.25$.
  </Step>
  <Step title="No successes">
    $P(X=0)=\binom{20}{0}(0.25)^0(0.75)^{20}=0.75^{20}\approx0.00317$.
  </Step>
  <Step title="At least one">
    Adding $P(X=1)+\cdots+P(X=20)$ is unnecessary. Its complement is $X=0$.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Subtract">
    $P(X\ge 1)=1-0.75^{20}\approx0.99683$.
  </Step>
</Steps>

**Final answer.** $\boxed{p=0.25,\quad P(X=0)\approx0.00317,\quad P(X\ge 1)\approx0.99683}$.

</details>

</ExamQuestion>

## Geometric

<ExamQuestion title="Foundation: First success on trial three">

A machine starts correctly on each attempt with probability $0.4$, independently of previous attempts. Let $X$ be the attempt number of the first correct start. Find $P(X=3)$.

<details>
<summary>Show the complete worked solution</summary>

<Steps>
  <Step title="Describe the required sequence">
    The first two attempts must fail and the third must succeed: $F,F,S$.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Attach probabilities">
    A failure has probability $0.6$, and the success has probability $0.4$.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Multiply independent trials">
    $P(X=3)=(0.6)^2(0.4)=0.144$.
  </Step>
</Steps>

**Final answer.** $\boxed{P(X=3)=0.144}$.

</details>

</ExamQuestion>

<ExamQuestion title="Developing: Success within four attempts">

A data packet is delivered successfully on each attempt with probability $0.25$, independently. Find the probability that the first successful delivery occurs within the first four attempts. Also find the expected number of attempts.

<details>
<summary>Show the complete worked solution</summary>

<Steps>
  <Step title="Translate “within four”">
    $P(X\le 4)=1-P(X>4)$.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Interpret the complement">
    $X>4$ means the first four attempts all fail, with probability $(0.75)^4$.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Calculate">
    $P(X\le 4)=1-(0.75)^4=0.68359375$.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Find the mean">
    $E[X]=\frac{1}{p}=\frac{1}{0.25}=4$ attempts.
  </Step>
</Steps>

**Final answer.** $\boxed{P(X\le 4)\approx0.6836,\quad E[X]=4}$.

</details>

</ExamQuestion>

<ExamQuestion title="Exam: Use memorylessness correctly">

A component passes a diagnostic on each independent attempt with probability $0.2$. After 5 unsuccessful attempts, what is the probability that more than 3 additional attempts are needed before the first pass? Explain why the first five failures do not appear in the final calculation.

<details>
<summary>Show the complete worked solution</summary>

<Steps>
  <Step title="Name the property">
    The geometric distribution is memoryless. Once the first five failures are known, the future wait behaves like a fresh geometric experiment.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Write the conditional event">
    $P(X>8\mid X>5)=P(X>3)$.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Calculate the fresh tail">
    $P(X>3)=(1-0.2)^3=(0.8)^3=0.512$.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Interpret">
    The known failures change neither the success probability nor the distribution of the remaining wait.
  </Step>
</Steps>

**Final answer.** $\boxed{0.512}$.

</details>

</ExamQuestion>

## Negative binomial

<ExamQuestion title="Foundation: Third success on trial five">

A player wins each independent round with probability $0.5$. Let $X$ be the round on which the player records the third win. Find $P(X=5)$.

<details>
<summary>Show the complete worked solution</summary>

<Steps>
  <Step title="Lock the last trial">
    Round 5 must be the third win.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Arrange earlier wins">
    Exactly 2 wins must occur among rounds 1–4, giving $\binom42$ arrangements.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Attach probabilities">
    Across all five rounds there are 3 wins and 2 losses: $(0.5)^3(0.5)^2$.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Multiply">
    $P(X=5)=\binom42(0.5)^5=\frac{6}{32}=0.1875$.
  </Step>
</Steps>

**Final answer.** $\boxed{P(X=5)=0.1875}$.

</details>

</ExamQuestion>

<ExamQuestion title="Developing: Mean and variance of the wait">

A recruiter hires each independently interviewed candidate with probability $0.2$. Interviews continue until 5 candidates have been hired. Let $X$ be the total number of interviews. Find $E[X]$, $\operatorname{Var}(X)$, and the standard deviation.

<details>
<summary>Show the complete worked solution</summary>

<Steps>
  <Step title="Identify parameters">
    $r=5$ hires and $p=0.2$ per interview.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Mean">
    $E[X]=\frac{r}{p}=\frac{5}{0.2}=25$.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Variance">
    $\operatorname{Var}(X)=\frac{r(1-p)}{p^2}=\frac{5(0.8)}{(0.2)^2}=100$.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Standard deviation">
    $\sigma_X=\sqrt{100}=10$ interviews.
  </Step>
</Steps>

**Final answer.** $\boxed{E[X]=25,\quad \operatorname{Var}(X)=100,\quad \sigma_X=10}$.

</details>

</ExamQuestion>

<ExamQuestion title="Exam: Distinguish the mirror-image models">

A basketball player scores independently with probability $0.6$. Compare the following events: A = “exactly 4 scores occur in the first 7 shots” and B = “the fourth score occurs on shot 7.” Name the correct distribution for each event, write both probabilities, and explain why they are not equal.

<details>
<summary>Show the complete worked solution</summary>

<Steps>
  <Step title="Event A: fixed trials">
    Seven shots are fixed and successes are counted, so $P(A)=\binom74(0.6)^4(0.4)^3$.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Event B: fixed success target">
    The fourth success is fixed on shot 7, so shot 7 is a success and the first six contain exactly three: $P(B)=\binom63(0.6)^4(0.4)^3$.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Compare the coefficients">
    $\binom74=35$ while $\binom63=20$. Event A allows the fourth success to occur anywhere by shot 7; event B forces it onto shot 7.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Evaluate">
    $P(A)\approx0.2903$, whereas $P(B)\approx0.1659$.
  </Step>
</Steps>

**Final answer.** $\boxed{P(A)=\binom74(0.6)^4(0.4)^3,\quad P(B)=\binom63(0.6)^4(0.4)^3}$.

</details>

</ExamQuestion>

## Poisson

<ExamQuestion title="Foundation: Exactly two arrivals">

A help desk receives an average of 3 independent requests per minute. Let $X$ be the number received in the next minute. Find the probability of exactly 2 requests and state the mean and variance.

<details>
<summary>Show the complete worked solution</summary>

<Steps>
  <Step title="Identify the interval mean">
    The stated rate already matches the one-minute window, so $\mu=3$.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Identify the requested count">
    “Exactly 2” means $k=2$ and requires one PMF term.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Substitute">
    $P(X=2)=\frac{e^{-3}3^2}{2!}$.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Evaluate and report moments">
    $P(X=2)\approx0.2240$, while $E[X]=\operatorname{Var}(X)=3$.
  </Step>
</Steps>

**Final answer.** $\boxed{P(X=2)\approx0.2240,\ E[X]=3,\ \operatorname{Var}(X)=3}$.

</details>

</ExamQuestion>

<ExamQuestion title="Developing: Rescale from an hourly rate">

Messages arrive at a stable average rate of 20 per hour. Assuming a Poisson model, find the probability that exactly 8 messages arrive during a 30-minute meeting. Identify every parameter before substituting.

<details>
<summary>Show the complete worked solution</summary>

<Steps>
  <Step title="Match the units">
    Thirty minutes is $\frac{30}{60}=0.5$ hour.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Build the interval mean">
    $\mu=(20\text{ per hour})(0.5\text{ hour})=10$. The number 20 is not the mean for this shorter window.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Read the requested count">
    Exactly eight messages gives $k=8$.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Substitute and evaluate">
    $P(X=8)=\frac{e^{-10}10^8}{8!}\approx0.1126$.
  </Step>
</Steps>

**Final answer.** $\boxed{\mu=10,\ k=8,\ P(X=8)\approx0.1126}$.

</details>

</ExamQuestion>

<ExamQuestion title="Exam: Convert seconds and calculate a cumulative probability">

A sensor records an average of 5 particle detections per minute. Detections follow a Poisson process. Find the probability of at most 2 detections during an 18-second exposure. Your solution must show the unit conversion, the new mean, and every PMF term in the cumulative probability.

<details>
<summary>Show the complete worked solution</summary>

<Steps>
  <Step title="Convert the exposure">
    $18$ seconds is $\frac{18}{60}=0.3$ minute.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Scale the mean">
    $\mu=5(0.3)=1.5$ expected detections in the exposure.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Translate “at most”">
    $P(X\le2)=P(X=0)+P(X=1)+P(X=2)$.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Factor and evaluate">
    $P(X\le2)=e^{-1.5}\left(1+1.5+\frac{1.5^2}{2}\right)=3.625e^{-1.5}\approx0.8088$.
  </Step>
</Steps>

**Final answer.** $\boxed{P(X\le2)\approx0.8088}$.

</details>

</ExamQuestion>
