## 6.2 The Normal Distribution and Standardization

The Normal (or Gaussian) distribution is the most important continuous distribution in statistics, serving as the foundation for inferential statistics and the Central Limit Theorem.

<Card>

### Definition 6.2.1 (Normal Distribution)

A random variable $X$ follows a **Normal Distribution** with mean $\mu$ and variance $\sigma^2$, denoted $X \sim N(\mu, \sigma^2)$, if its PDF is:
$$
f(x) = \frac{1}{\sigma \sqrt{2\pi}} e^{-\frac{1}{2} \left( \frac{x-\mu}{\sigma} \right)^2} \quad \text{for } -\infty < x < \infty
$$

</Card>

<HighlightCard
  variant="neutral"
  title="Theorem 6.2.1: Standardization (Z-Score)"
  description="Any Normal random variable can be transformed into the Standard Normal distribution $Z \sim N(0, 1)$ via a linear transformation."
>

$$
Z = \frac{X - \mu}{\sigma}
$$

This transformation shifts the mean to $0$ and scales the standard deviation to $1$, allowing the use of standard Normal (Z) tables to compute probabilities.

</HighlightCard>

<HighlightCard
  variant="neutral"
  title="Theorem 6.2.2: The Symmetry Axiom of the Normal Distribution"
  description="Because the Standard Normal PDF is perfectly symmetric about $z = 0$, probabilities in the left tail mirror those in the right tail."
>

For any $z \in \mathbb{R}$:
$$
P(Z < -z) = P(Z > z) = 1 - P(Z < z)
$$
Additionally, the probability of falling within a symmetric interval is:
$$
P(-z < Z < z) = 2P(Z < z) - 1
$$

</HighlightCard>

<StepByStep>
  <StepByStepTitle>Example: Forward Z-Table Problem (Finding Probability)</StepByStepTitle>

  <Step number={1}>
    <StepTitle>Problem Statement</StepTitle>

    The lifetimes of a certain brand of lightbulb are normally distributed with a mean of $\mu = 1200$ hours and a standard deviation of $\sigma = 100$ hours. What is the probability that a randomly selected bulb lasts between 1100 and 1350 hours?
  </Step>

  <Step number={2}>
    <StepTitle>Standardize the Bounds</StepTitle>

    For $x_1 = 1100$: $z_1 = \frac{1100 - 1200}{100} = -1.00$
    For $x_2 = 1350$: $z_2 = \frac{1350 - 1200}{100} = 1.50$
  </Step>

  <Step number={3}>
    <StepTitle>Express in Terms of Z</StepTitle>

    $P(1100 < X < 1350) = P(-1.00 < Z < 1.50)$
    $P(-1.00 < Z < 1.50) = P(Z < 1.50) - P(Z < -1.00)$
  </Step>

  <Step number={4}>
    <StepTitle>Lookup in Z-Table and Calculate</StepTitle>

    From standard Z-tables: $P(Z < 1.50) \approx 0.9332$ and $P(Z < -1.00) \approx 0.1587$.
    Probability $= 0.9332 - 0.1587 = 0.7745$.
  </Step>
</StepByStep>

<StepByStep>
  <StepByStepTitle>Example: Inverse Z-Table Problem (Finding a Value)</StepByStepTitle>

  <Step number={1}>
    <StepTitle>Problem Statement</StepTitle>

    Using the same lightbulb distribution ($\mu = 1200, \sigma = 100$), find the lifetime $x$ such that only 5% of bulbs last longer than $x$.
  </Step>

  <Step number={2}>
    <StepTitle>Formulate the Probability Statement</StepTitle>

    We need $x$ such that $P(X > x) = 0.05$, which is equivalent to the cumulative probability $P(X \le x) = 0.95$.
  </Step>

  <Step number={3}>
    <StepTitle>Find the Corresponding Z-Score</StepTitle>

    Look up the cumulative probability $0.9500$ in the body of the standard Z-table. The closest value corresponds to $z \approx 1.645$.
  </Step>

  <Step number={4}>
    <StepTitle>Un-standardize to Find x</StepTitle>

    Using $z = \frac{x - \mu}{\sigma}$, we solve for $x$:
    $x = \mu + z\sigma = 1200 + (1.645)(100) = 1200 + 164.5 = 1364.5$ hours.
  </Step>
</StepByStep>
