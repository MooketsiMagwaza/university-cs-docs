## 6.1 Continuous Uniform and Exponential Distributions

Continuous distributions model random variables that can take any value within an interval. We begin with the simplest continuous model and progress to the distribution that governs waiting times and lifespans.

<Card>

### Definition 6.1.1 (Continuous Uniform Distribution)

A random variable $X$ follows a **Continuous Uniform Distribution** on the interval $[a, b]$, denoted $X \sim U(a, b)$, if all sub-intervals of equal length within $[a, b]$ are equally likely.

**Probability Density Function (PDF):**
$$
f(x) = 
\begin{cases} 
\frac{1}{b-a} & \text{for } a \le x \le b \\
0 & \text{otherwise}
\end{cases}
$$

**Cumulative Distribution Function (CDF):**
$$
F(x) = 
\begin{cases} 
0 & x < a \\
\frac{x-a}{b-a} & a \le x \le b \\
1 & x > b
\end{cases}
$$

</Card>

<HighlightCard
  variant="neutral"
  title="Theorem 6.1.1: Moments of the Uniform Distribution"
  description="The expectation and variance of a Uniform random variable depend solely on the interval endpoints."
>

For $X \sim U(a, b)$:
$$
E[X] = \frac{a+b}{2} \quad \text{and} \quad V[X] = \frac{(b-a)^2}{12}
$$

</HighlightCard>

<Card>

### Definition 6.1.2 (Exponential Distribution)

A random variable $X$ follows an **Exponential Distribution** with rate parameter $\lambda > 0$, denoted $X \sim \text{Exp}(\lambda)$, if it models the time between events in a Poisson process.

**Probability Density Function (PDF):**
$$
f(x) = 
\begin{cases} 
\lambda e^{-\lambda x} & \text{for } x \ge 0 \\
0 & \text{for } x < 0
\end{cases}
$$

**Cumulative Distribution Function (CDF):**
$$
F(x) = P(X \le x) = 1 - e^{-\lambda x} \quad \text{for } x \ge 0
$$

</Card>

<HighlightCard
  variant="neutral"
  title="Theorem 6.1.2: Moments of the Exponential Distribution"
  description="The mean and variance of the Exponential distribution are directly determined by the rate parameter $\lambda$."
>

For $X \sim \text{Exp}(\lambda)$:
$$
E[X] = \frac{1}{\lambda} \quad \text{and} \quad V[X] = \frac{1}{\lambda^2}
$$

</HighlightCard>

<HighlightCard
  variant="neutral"
  title="Theorem 6.1.3: The Memoryless Property"
  description="The Exponential distribution is the only continuous distribution that possesses the memoryless property. The probability of an event occurring in the future is independent of how much time has already elapsed."
>

For any $s, t \ge 0$:
$$
P(X > s + t \mid X > s) = P(X > t)
$$

</HighlightCard>

<StepByStep>
  <StepByStepTitle>Proof: The Memoryless Property of the Exponential Distribution</StepByStepTitle>

  <Step number={1}>
    <StepTitle>Apply the Definition of Conditional Probability</StepTitle>

    $P(X > s + t \mid X > s) = \frac{P(X > s + t \text{ and } X > s)}{P(X > s)}$
  </Step>

  <Step number={2}>
    <StepTitle>Simplify the Intersection</StepTitle>

    Since $t \ge 0$, the event $\{X > s + t\}$ is a subset of $\{X > s\}$. Therefore, their intersection is simply $\{X > s + t\}$.
    $P(X > s + t \mid X > s) = \frac{P(X > s + t)}{P(X > s)}$
  </Step>

  <Step number={3}>
    <StepTitle>Substitute the Survival Function</StepTitle>

    The survival function is $P(X > x) = 1 - F(x) = e^{-\lambda x}$.
    Substituting this yields:
    $\frac{e^{-\lambda(s+t)}}{e^{-\lambda s}}$
  </Step>

  <Step number={4}>
    <StepTitle>Simplify the Exponent</StepTitle>

    Using exponent rules: $\frac{e^{-\lambda s} \cdot e^{-\lambda t}}{e^{-\lambda s}} = e^{-\lambda t} = P(X > t)$. $\blacksquare$
  </Step>
</StepByStep>
