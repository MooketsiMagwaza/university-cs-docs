## PDFs, CDFs and moments

<ExamQuestion title="Question 1: Normalisation, probabilities and moments [24 marks]">
  <SubQuestion
    part="a"
    prompt={<>A continuous random variable has PDF $f(x)=kx(3-x)$ for $0\le x\le3$, and $f(x)=0$ otherwise. Find $k$. [5]</>}
    solution={<>A PDF must have total area 1. Therefore $1=\int_0^3kx(3-x)\,dx=k\int_0^3(3x-x^2)\,dx=k[\frac32x^2-\frac13x^3]_0^3=k(\frac{27}{2}-9)=\frac92k$. Hence $\boxed{k=\frac29}$. The factor $x(3-x)$ is non-negative on $[0,3]$, so both PDF conditions hold. [5]</>}
  />
  <SubQuestion
    part="b"
    prompt={<>Calculate $P(1<X<2)$ and explain why the same answer is obtained for $P(1\le X\le2)$. [5]</>}
    solution={<>$P(1<X<2)=\int_1^2\frac29x(3-x)\,dx=\frac29[\frac32x^2-\frac13x^3]_1^2=\frac29[(6-\frac83)-(\frac32-\frac13)]=\frac29(\frac{13}{6})=\boxed{\frac{13}{27}\approx0.4815}$. Since $X$ is continuous, $P(X=1)=P(X=2)=0$, so including either endpoint cannot change the probability. [5]</>}
  />
  <SubQuestion
    part="c"
    prompt={<>Derive the CDF $F(x)$, including all branches. [6]</>}
    solution={<>For $x<0$, $F(x)=0$. For $0\le x\le3$, $F(x)=\int_0^x\frac29t(3-t)dt=\frac29[\frac32t^2-\frac13t^3]_0^x=\frac{x^2}{3}-\frac{2x^3}{27}$. For $x>3$, all probability has accumulated, so $F(x)=1$. Thus $\boxed{F(x)=0\ (x<0);\ F(x)=x^2/3-2x^3/27\ (0\le x\le3);\ F(x)=1\ (x>3)}$. [6]</>}
  />
  <SubQuestion
    part="d"
    prompt={<>Find $E[X]$ and $\operatorname{Var}(X)$. [8]</>}
    solution={<>$E[X]=\int_0^3x\frac29x(3-x)dx=\frac29\int_0^3(3x^2-x^3)dx=\frac29[x^3-\frac14x^4]_0^3=\frac29(27-\frac{81}{4})=\boxed{\frac32}$. Next, $E[X^2]=\frac29\int_0^3(3x^3-x^4)dx=\frac29[\frac34x^4-\frac15x^5]_0^3=\frac29(\frac{243}{4}-\frac{243}{5})=\frac{27}{10}$. Therefore $\operatorname{Var}(X)=\frac{27}{10}-(\frac32)^2=\frac{54-45}{20}=\boxed{\frac9{20}}$. Symmetry of $x(3-x)$ about $1.5$ also supports the mean. [8]</>}
  />
</ExamQuestion>

## Uniform distribution

<ExamQuestion title="Question 2: Uniform arrival time [14 marks]">
  <SubQuestion
    part="a"
    prompt={<>A delivery arrives at a time $X$ uniformly distributed between 10:00 and 10:30. Measure $X$ in minutes after 10:00. State the PDF and CDF. [5]</>}
    solution={<>$X\sim U(0,30)$. The PDF is $f(x)=1/30$ for $0\le x\le30$ and 0 otherwise. The CDF is $F(x)=0$ for $x<0$, $F(x)=x/30$ for $0\le x\le30$, and $F(x)=1$ for $x>30$. [5]</>}
  />
  <SubQuestion
    part="b"
    prompt={<>Find the probability that the delivery arrives between 10:08 and 10:20. [3]</>}
    solution={<>The desired interval has width $20-8=12$ minutes, while the support has width 30. Thus $P(8<X<20)=12/30=\boxed{0.4}$. [3]</>}
  />
  <SubQuestion
    part="c"
    prompt={<>Given that it has not arrived by 10:12, find the probability that it arrives by 10:20. Does this show memorylessness? [6]</>}
    solution={<>The condition restricts the possible arrival time to the remaining uniform interval $(12,30)$. Therefore $P(X\le20\mid X>12)=P(12<X\le20)/P(X>12)=(8/30)/(18/30)=\boxed{4/9\approx0.4444}$. This is not the same as the original $P(X\le8)=8/30$, and uniform waiting times are not memoryless. Only the exponential distribution has the continuous memoryless property. [6]</>}
  />
</ExamQuestion>

## Exponential distribution

<ExamQuestion title="Question 3: Network waiting time [20 marks]">
  <SubQuestion
    part="a"
    prompt={<>Requests arrive according to a Poisson process at an average rate of 6 per hour. Let $X$ be the waiting time in minutes until the next request. State the distribution and its parameter in matching units. [4]</>}
    solution={<>Six per hour equals $6/60=0.1$ per minute. The waiting time is therefore $\boxed{X\sim\operatorname{Exp}(0.1)}$ with $X$ in minutes. Its mean is $1/0.1=10$ minutes. [4]</>}
  />
  <SubQuestion
    part="b"
    prompt={<>Find the probability that the next request arrives within 7 minutes. [4]</>}
    solution={<>“Within 7” is a left cumulative probability: $P(X\le7)=1-e^{-0.1(7)}=1-e^{-0.7}=\boxed{0.5034}$ to four decimals. [4]</>}
  />
  <SubQuestion
    part="c"
    prompt={<>Find the probability that the wait is between 5 and 12 minutes. [5]</>}
    solution={<>$P(5<X<12)=e^{-0.1(5)}-e^{-0.1(12)}=e^{-0.5}-e^{-1.2}=0.6065-0.3010=\boxed{0.3055}$. [5]</>}
  />
  <SubQuestion
    part="d"
    prompt={<>You have already waited 15 minutes. Find the probability of waiting at least another 5 minutes, and name the property used. [4]</>}
    solution={<>By memorylessness, $P(X>20\mid X>15)=P(X>5)=e^{-0.1(5)}=\boxed{0.6065}$. The elapsed 15 minutes do not change the distribution of the additional exponential wait. [4]</>}
  />
  <SubQuestion
    part="e"
    prompt={<>Find the time $t$ such that 90% of requests arrive by time $t$. [3]</>}
    solution={<>Set $P(X\le t)=0.90$: $1-e^{-0.1t}=0.90$, so $e^{-0.1t}=0.10$ and $t=-\ln(0.10)/0.1=\boxed{23.026\text{ minutes}}$. [3]</>}
  />
</ExamQuestion>

## Normal probabilities

<ExamQuestion title="Question 4: Manufacturing dimensions [18 marks]">
  <SubQuestion
    part="a"
    prompt={<>A component diameter $X$ is normally distributed with mean 50 mm and standard deviation 2 mm. Find $P(X<47)$. Use $\Phi(-1.50)=0.0668$. [5]</>}
    solution={<>$X\sim N(50,2^2)$. Standardise: $z=(47-50)/2=-1.50$. Hence $P(X<47)=P(Z<-1.50)=\boxed{0.0668}$. [5]</>}
  />
  <SubQuestion
    part="b"
    prompt={<>Find $P(49<X<53)$. Use $\Phi(-0.50)=0.3085$ and $\Phi(1.50)=0.9332$. [6]</>}
    solution={<>The Z-scores are $(49-50)/2=-0.50$ and $(53-50)/2=1.50$. Therefore $P(49<X<53)=\Phi(1.50)-\Phi(-0.50)=0.9332-0.3085=\boxed{0.6247}$. [6]</>}
  />
  <SubQuestion
    part="c"
    prompt={<>Components outside the interval 46 mm to 54 mm are rejected. Find the rejection proportion. Use $\Phi(2.00)=0.9772$. [7]</>}
    solution={<>The bounds are $z=-2$ and $z=2$. The acceptance probability is $P(-2<Z<2)=2\Phi(2)-1=2(0.9772)-1=0.9544$. Therefore the rejection probability is $1-0.9544=\boxed{0.0456}$, or 4.56%. Symmetry also gives two tails of $0.0228$ each. [7]</>}
  />
</ExamQuestion>

## Inverse normal and transformations

<ExamQuestion title="Question 5: Cut-offs and unit changes [17 marks]">
  <SubQuestion
    part="a"
    prompt={<>Test scores are $X\sim N(68,10^2)$. Admission is offered to the highest 15% of candidates. Find the minimum score, using $z=1.036$ for left area 0.85. [6]</>}
    solution={<>Highest 15% means 15% lies to the right, so the left-tail area is $0.85$. Un-standardise: $x=\mu+z\sigma=68+1.036(10)=\boxed{78.36}$. A practical whole-number cut-off would depend on the institution's rounding rule. The positive Z-score correctly places the threshold above the mean. [6]</>}
  />
  <SubQuestion
    part="b"
    prompt={<>Find the symmetric interval containing the central 90% of scores, using $z=1.645$. [5]</>}
    solution={<>Central 90% leaves 5% in each tail, so the bounds are $68\pm1.645(10)$. Thus the interval is $\boxed{51.55<X<84.45}$. [5]</>}
  />
  <SubQuestion
    part="c"
    prompt={<>A temperature $C\sim N(25,4^2)$ degrees Celsius is converted to Fahrenheit by $F=\frac95C+32$. Find the distribution of $F$. [6]</>}
    solution={<>A linear transformation preserves normality. The new mean is $E[F]=\frac95(25)+32=77$. The new standard deviation is $|\frac95|4=7.2$, so the variance is $7.2^2=51.84$. Therefore $\boxed{F\sim N(77,51.84)}$. [6]</>}
  />
</ExamQuestion>

## Reliability application

<ExamQuestion title="Question 6: Components in series and parallel [15 marks]">
  <SubQuestion
    part="a"
    prompt={<>Three independent components have reliabilities 0.95, 0.90 and 0.80 for a mission. Find the reliability if all three are required. [4]</>}
    solution={<>This is a series system, so all components must work: $R=0.95(0.90)(0.80)=\boxed{0.684}$. [4]</>}
  />
  <SubQuestion
    part="b"
    prompt={<>Find the reliability if the system succeeds when at least one of the three components works. [5]</>}
    solution={<>Use the complement of all three failing: $R=1-(0.05)(0.10)(0.20)=1-0.001=\boxed{0.999}$. [5]</>}
  />
  <SubQuestion
    part="c"
    prompt={<>A component lifetime is exponential with mean 200 hours. Find its reliability for a 50-hour mission and its hazard rate. [6]</>}
    solution={<>$\lambda=1/200=0.005$ per hour. The reliability is $R(50)=e^{-0.005(50)}=e^{-0.25}=\boxed{0.7788}$. For an exponential lifetime, the hazard is constant: $\boxed{h(t)=\lambda=0.005\text{ per hour}}$. [6]</>}
  />
</ExamQuestion>
