## Question 1: Normalisation, probabilities and moments [24 marks]

A continuous random variable has PDF

$$
f(x)=
\begin{cases}
kx(3-x),&0\le x\le3,\\
0,&\text{otherwise}.
\end{cases}
$$

1. Find $k$. [5]
2. Calculate $P(1\lt X\lt2)$ and explain why the same answer is obtained with inclusive endpoints. [5]
3. Derive the complete CDF $F(x)$. [6]
4. Find $E[X]$ and $\operatorname{Var}(X)$. [8]

<Accordions>
<Accordion title="Show the complete worked solution">

**1. Normalise the PDF**

The total area must equal 1:

$$
1=\int_0^3kx(3-x)\,dx
=k\left[\frac32x^2-\frac13x^3\right]_0^3
=\frac92k.
$$

Therefore

$$\boxed{k=\frac29}.$$

The factor $x(3-x)$ is non-negative on $[0,3]$, so the non-negativity condition also holds.

**2. Interval probability**

$$
P(1\lt X\lt2)
=\int_1^2\frac29x(3-x)\,dx
=\frac29\left[\frac32x^2-\frac13x^3\right]_1^2
=\frac29\left(\frac{13}{6}\right)
=\boxed{\frac{13}{27}\approx0.4815}.
$$

Because $X$ is continuous, $P(X=1)=P(X=2)=0$. Including either endpoint cannot change the area.

**3. CDF**

For $0\le x\le3$,

$$
F(x)=\int_0^x\frac29t(3-t)\,dt
=\frac{x^2}{3}-\frac{2x^3}{27}.
$$

Thus

$$
F(x)=
\begin{cases}
0,&x\lt0,\\[4pt]
\dfrac{x^2}{3}-\dfrac{2x^3}{27},&0\le x\le3,\\[8pt]
1,&x\gt3.
\end{cases}
$$

**4. Mean and variance**

$$
E[X]=\int_0^3x\frac29x(3-x)\,dx
=\frac29\left[x^3-\frac14x^4\right]_0^3
=\boxed{\frac32}.
$$

$$
E[X^2]=\int_0^3x^2\frac29x(3-x)\,dx
=\frac29\left[\frac34x^4-\frac15x^5\right]_0^3
=\frac{27}{10}.
$$

Therefore

$$
\operatorname{Var}(X)=\frac{27}{10}-\left(\frac32\right)^2
=\boxed{\frac9{20}}.
$$

</Accordion>
</Accordions>

## Question 2: Uniform arrival time [14 marks]

A delivery arrives at a time $X$ uniformly distributed between 10:00 and 10:30. Measure $X$ in minutes after 10:00.

1. State the PDF and CDF. [5]
2. Find the probability that the delivery arrives between 10:08 and 10:20. [3]
3. Given that it has not arrived by 10:12, find the probability that it arrives by 10:20. Does this demonstrate memorylessness? [6]

<Accordions>
<Accordion title="Show the complete worked solution">

$X\sim U(0,30)$, so

$$
f(x)=
\begin{cases}
1/30,&0\le x\le30,\\
0,&\text{otherwise},
\end{cases}
$$

and

$$
F(x)=
\begin{cases}
0,&x\lt0,\\
x/30,&0\le x\le30,\\
1,&x\gt30.
\end{cases}
$$

The interval from 8 to 20 has width 12:

$$P(8\lt X\lt20)=\frac{12}{30}=\boxed{0.4}.$$

For the conditional probability,

$$
P(X\le20\mid X\gt12)
=\frac{P(12\lt X\le20)}{P(X\gt12)}
=\frac{8/30}{18/30}
=\boxed{\frac49\approx0.4444}.
$$

This is not memorylessness. A uniform wait changes when part of its finite support has elapsed; the exponential is the continuous memoryless distribution.

</Accordion>
</Accordions>

## Question 3: Network waiting time [20 marks]

Requests arrive according to a Poisson process at an average rate of 6 per hour. Let $X$ be the waiting time in minutes until the next request.

1. State the distribution and parameter in matching units. [4]
2. Find the probability that the next request arrives within 7 minutes. [4]
3. Find the probability that the wait is between 5 and 12 minutes. [5]
4. You have already waited 15 minutes. Find the probability of waiting at least another 5 minutes, and name the property used. [4]
5. Find the time $t$ such that 90% of requests arrive by time $t$. [3]

<Accordions>
<Accordion title="Show the complete worked solution">

Six per hour equals $6/60=0.1$ per minute, so

$$\boxed{X\sim\operatorname{Exp}(0.1)}.$$

Its mean is $1/0.1=10$ minutes.

“Within 7” is a left cumulative probability:

$$
P(X\le7)=1-e^{-0.1(7)}
=\boxed{0.5034}.
$$

For the interval,

$$
P(5\lt X\lt12)=e^{-0.1(5)}-e^{-0.1(12)}
=0.6065-0.3010
=\boxed{0.3055}.
$$

By memorylessness,

$$
P(X\gt20\mid X\gt15)=P(X\gt5)
=e^{-0.5}
=\boxed{0.6065}.
$$

Finally, set the CDF equal to $0.90$:

$$
1-e^{-0.1t}=0.90
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
t=-\frac{\ln(0.10)}{0.1}
=\boxed{23.026\text{ minutes}}.
$$

</Accordion>
</Accordions>

## Question 4: Manufacturing dimensions [18 marks]

A component diameter is $X\sim N(50,2^2)$ millimetres.

1. Find $P(X\lt47)$. Use $\Phi(-1.50)=0.0668$. [5]
2. Find $P(49\lt X\lt53)$. Use $\Phi(-0.50)=0.3085$ and $\Phi(1.50)=0.9332$. [6]
3. Components outside 46 mm to 54 mm are rejected. Find the rejection proportion. Use $\Phi(2.00)=0.9772$. [7]

<Accordions>
<Accordion title="Show the complete worked solution">

For 47 mm,

$$z=\frac{47-50}{2}=-1.50,$$

so

$$P(X\lt47)=\boxed{0.0668}.$$

For 49 and 53 mm,

$$z_1=\frac{49-50}{2}=-0.50,\qquad z_2=\frac{53-50}{2}=1.50.$$

Therefore

$$
P(49\lt X\lt53)=\Phi(1.50)-\Phi(-0.50)
=0.9332-0.3085
=\boxed{0.6247}.
$$

The rejection limits have Z-scores $-2$ and $2$. The accepted central area is

$$P(-2\lt Z\lt2)=2\Phi(2)-1=2(0.9772)-1=0.9544.$$

Thus

$$P(\text{reject})=1-0.9544=\boxed{0.0456},$$

or $4.56\%$.

</Accordion>
</Accordions>

## Question 5: Cut-offs and transformations [17 marks]

Test scores are $X\sim N(68,10^2)$.

1. Admission is offered to the highest 15% of candidates. Find the minimum score, using $z=1.036$ for left area 0.85. [6]
2. Find the symmetric interval containing the central 90% of scores, using $z=1.645$. [5]
3. Separately, a temperature $C\sim N(25,4^2)$ is converted by $F=\frac95C+32$. Find the distribution of $F$. [6]

<Accordions>
<Accordion title="Show the complete worked solution">

Highest 15% means 85% lies to the left of the cut-off. Hence

$$x=\mu+z\sigma=68+1.036(10)=\boxed{78.36}.$$

For the central 90%, $5\%$ lies in each tail. The bounds are

$$68\pm1.645(10),$$

giving

$$\boxed{51.55\lt X\lt84.45}.$$

A linear transformation preserves normality. For Fahrenheit,

$$E[F]=\frac95(25)+32=77,$$

and

$$\operatorname{Var}(F)=\left(\frac95\right)^2(4^2)=51.84.$$

Therefore

$$\boxed{F\sim N(77,51.84)}.$$

</Accordion>
