## Random variables

<ExamQuestion title="Foundation: Build a random variable from a sample space">

A fair coin is tossed twice. Let $X$ be the number of heads observed. List the sample space, apply the rule $X$ to every outcome, state the support of $X$, and identify the event $\{X=1\}$.

<details>
<summary>Show the complete worked solution</summary>

<Steps>
  <Step title="Write the outcomes">
    $\Omega=\{HH,HT,TH,TT\}$. The experiment produces one of these four strings.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Apply the rule">
    $X(HH)=2, X(HT)=1, X(TH)=1, X(TT)=0$.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Collect the possible values">
    The support is $S_X=\{0,1,2\}$. Notice that the support contains numbers, not coin strings.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Translate the requested event">
    $\{X=1\}=\{HT,TH\}$, because those are exactly the outcomes containing one head.
  </Step>
</Steps>

**Final answer.** $S_X=\{0,1,2\}$ and $\{X=1\}=\{HT,TH\}$.

</details>

</ExamQuestion>

<ExamQuestion title="Developing: Classify counts and measurements">

For each variable, state whether it is discrete or continuous and justify the decision: (i) the number of support tickets received in an hour, (ii) the exact time between two ticket arrivals, (iii) the number of urgent tickets among the next 10, and (iv) the exact duration of a support call.

<details>
<summary>Show the complete worked solution</summary>

<Steps>
  <Step title="Variable (i)">
    Ticket arrivals are counted in whole numbers, so the number received is discrete.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Variable (ii)">
    Elapsed time is measured and may take any nonnegative real value, so it is continuous.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Variable (iii)">
    Urgent tickets are counted from 0 through 10, so the variable is discrete.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Variable (iv)">
    Call duration is measured on a time interval, so it is continuous.
  </Step>
  <Step title="State the general rule">
    Counts are discrete; measurements across intervals are continuous.
  </Step>
</Steps>

**Final answer.** $\boxed{\text{discrete, continuous, discrete, continuous}}$.

</details>

</ExamQuestion>

<ExamQuestion title="Exam: Several random variables on one experiment">

Two fair dice are rolled and a stopwatch measures the exact time taken for the dice to stop. Define $X$ as the sum of the faces, $Y$ as the larger face, and $T$ as the measured stopping time. For the outcome $\left(2,5\right)$, find $X$ and $Y$, describe the event $\{X=7\}$, state the supports of $X$ and $Y$, and classify all three variables.

<details>
<summary>Show the complete worked solution</summary>

<Steps>
  <Step title="Apply both rules">
    For $\left(2,5\right)$, the sum is $X=2+5=7$, while the larger face is $Y=5$.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Describe the sum event">
    $\{X=7\}=\{(1,6),(2,5),(3,4),(4,3),(5,2),(6,1)\}$.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Find the sum support">
    The smallest sum is $2$, the largest is $12$, and every integer between them is possible: $S_X=\{2,3,\ldots,12\}$.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Find the maximum support">
    The larger die can be any integer from $1$ to $6$: $S_Y=\{1,2,\ldots,6\}$.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Classify the variables">
    $X$ and $Y$ are discrete because they take separated integer values. $T$ is continuous because exact stopping time is measured along an interval.
  </Step>
</Steps>

**Final answer.** $X=7$ and $Y=5$ for $\left(2,5\right)$; $X,Y$ are discrete, while $T$ is continuous.

</details>

</ExamQuestion>

## PMFs

<ExamQuestion title="Foundation: Check a proposed PMF">

A discrete random variable has support $\{0,1,2,3\}$ and proposed masses $0.10,0.25,0.40,0.25$, respectively. Determine whether this is a valid PMF and justify both validity conditions explicitly.

<details>
<summary>Show the complete worked solution</summary>

<Steps>
  <Step title="Check each mass">
    All four values are at least zero.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Check the total">
    $0.10+0.25+0.40+0.25=1.00$.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Conclude">
    Both PMF axioms hold, so the proposal is valid.
  </Step>
</Steps>

**Final answer.** The proposed PMF is $\boxed{\text{valid}}$.

</details>

</ExamQuestion>

<ExamQuestion title="Developing: Normalise a formula">

Let $X$ have support $\{1,2,3,4\}$ and PMF $p_X(x)=cx$. Find the value of $c$ that makes this a valid PMF. Then calculate $P(X\le 2)$.

<details>
<summary>Show the complete worked solution</summary>

<Steps>
  <Step title="Force total mass to one">
    $1=\sum_{x=1}^{4}cx=c(1+2+3+4)=10c$.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Solve for the constant">
    $c=\frac1{10}=0.1$. All masses are nonnegative because $x>0$.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Translate the event">
    $P(X\le 2)=p_X(1)+p_X(2)$.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Substitute">
    $0.1(1)+0.1(2)=0.3$.
  </Step>
</Steps>

**Final answer.** $\boxed{c=0.1,\quad P(X\le 2)=0.3}$.

</details>

</ExamQuestion>

<ExamQuestion title="Exam: Build the PMF from outcomes">

Two fair dice are rolled and $X$ is the number of sixes observed. Construct the PMF of $X$, prove that it is valid, and find the probability that at least one six is observed.

<details>
<summary>Show the complete worked solution</summary>

<Steps>
  <Step title="State the support">
    $X\in\{0,1,2\}$.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Calculate each mass">
    $P(X=0)=\left(\frac{5}{6}\right)^2=\frac{25}{36}$, $P(X=1)=\binom21\left(\frac{1}{6}\right)\left(\frac{5}{6}\right)=\frac{10}{36}$, and $P(X=2)=\left(\frac{1}{6}\right)^2=\frac{1}{36}$.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Prove validity">
    Every mass is nonnegative and $\frac{25+10+1}{36}=1$.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Use the complement">
    $P(X\ge 1)=1-P(X=0)=1-\frac{25}{36}=\frac{11}{36}$.
  </Step>
</Steps>

**Final answer.** $p_X(0)=\frac{25}{36}, p_X(1)=\frac{10}{36}, p_X(2)=\frac{1}{36}$, and $\boxed{P(X\ge 1)=\frac{11}{36}}$.

</details>

</ExamQuestion>

## CDFs

<ExamQuestion title="Foundation: Read a cumulative probability">

A variable has $p_X(0)=0.20$, $p_X(1)=0.50$, and $p_X(2)=0.30$. Find $F_X(1)$ and explain what the value means in words.

<details>
<summary>Show the complete worked solution</summary>

<Steps>
  <Step title="Use the definition">
    $F_X(1)=P(X\le 1)$.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Accumulate the masses">
    $F_X(1)=p_X(0)+p_X(1)=0.20+0.50=0.70$.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Interpret">
    There is a 70% probability that $X$ is no larger than 1.
  </Step>
</Steps>

**Final answer.** $\boxed{F_X(1)=0.70}$.

</details>

</ExamQuestion>

<ExamQuestion title="Developing: Evaluate between support points">

The support of $X$ is $\{1,3,5\}$ with masses $0.25,0.45,0.30$. Find $F_X(4.8)$ and $P(1<X\le 5)$.

<details>
<summary>Show the complete worked solution</summary>

<Steps>
  <Step title="Locate 4.8">
    Only support values 1 and 3 are at or below 4.8.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Accumulate">
    $F_X(4.8)=0.25+0.45=0.70$. The CDF is flat between 3 and 5.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Use the interval rule">
    $P(1<X\le 5)=F_X(5)-F_X(1)=1-0.25=0.75$.
  </Step>
</Steps>

**Final answer.** $\boxed{F_X(4.8)=0.70,\quad P(1<X\le 5)=0.75}$.

</details>

</ExamQuestion>

<ExamQuestion title="Exam: Recover the PMF from a staircase">

A discrete CDF satisfies $F_X(x)=0$ for $x<0$, $F_X(x)=0.15$ for $0\le  x<1$, $F_X(x)=0.55$ for $1\le  x<3$, and $F_X(x)=1$ for $x\ge3$. Determine the support and the complete PMF.

<details>
<summary>Show the complete worked solution</summary>

<Steps>
  <Step title="Find the jump locations">
    The CDF changes at $x=0,1,3$, so the support is $\{0,1,3\}$.
  </Step>
  <Step title="First jump">
    $p_X(0)=0.15-0=0.15$.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Second jump">
    $p_X(1)=0.55-0.15=0.40$.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Final jump">
    $p_X(3)=1-0.55=0.45$. The masses total 1.
  </Step>
</Steps>

**Final answer.** $\boxed{p_X(0)=0.15, p_X(1)=0.40, p_X(3)=0.45}$.

</details>

</ExamQuestion>

## Expectation

<ExamQuestion title="Foundation: Find a weighted mean">

A game pays $X=0$ pula with probability $0.50$, $X=20$ pula with probability $0.40$, and $X=100$ pula with probability $0.10$. Find the expected payout and explain why the answer is not the most likely payout.

<details>
<summary>Show the complete worked solution</summary>

<Steps>
  <Step title="Set up the weighted average">
    $E[X]=0(0.50)+20(0.40)+100(0.10)$.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Compute the contributions">
    The contributions are $0$, $8$, and $10$.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Add">
    $E[X]=18$ pula.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Interpret correctly">
    The most likely single payout is 0, but the long-run average over many plays is 18.
  </Step>
</Steps>

**Final answer.** $\boxed{E[X]=18\text{ pula}}$.

</details>

</ExamQuestion>

<ExamQuestion title="Developing: Use E[g(X)]">

The number of failed checks in a batch has PMF $p_X(0)=0.5$, $p_X(1)=0.3$, $p_X(2)=0.2$. The repair cost is $g(X)=50+30X^2$ pula. Find the expected repair cost.

<details>
<summary>Show the complete worked solution</summary>

<Steps>
  <Step title="Transform each value">
    $g(0)=50, g(1)=80, g(2)=170$.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Weight the transformed values">
    $E[g(X)]=50(0.5)+80(0.3)+170(0.2)$.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Add the contributions">
    $25+24+34=83$.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Avoid the common error">
    Do not use $g(E[X])$; $g$ is nonlinear.
  </Step>
</Steps>

**Final answer.** $\boxed{E[g(X)]=83\text{ pula}}$.

</details>

</ExamQuestion>

<ExamQuestion title="Exam: Infer a missing mass and then average">

A discrete variable has support $\{-2,0,1,4\}$. Its PMF values are $0.10$, $a$, $0.35$, and $0.20$, respectively. First determine $a$. Then find $E[X]$ and $E[3X-5]$.

<details>
<summary>Show the complete worked solution</summary>

<Steps>
  <Step title="Normalise the PMF">
    $0.10+a+0.35+0.20=1$, so $a=0.35$.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Calculate the mean">
    $E[X]=(-2)(0.10)+0(0.35)+1(0.35)+4(0.20)=0.95$.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Use linearity">
    $E[3X-5]=3E[X]-5=3(0.95)-5$.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Simplify">
    $2.85-5=-2.15$.
  </Step>
</Steps>

**Final answer.** $\boxed{a=0.35, E[X]=0.95, E[3X-5]=-2.15}$.

</details>

</ExamQuestion>

## Variance

<ExamQuestion title="Foundation: Use the shortcut formula">

A variable has $E[X]=1.1$ and $E[X^2]=1.7$. Find $\operatorname{Var}(X)$ and the standard deviation.

<details>
<summary>Show the complete worked solution</summary>

<Steps>
  <Step title="Write the shortcut">
    $\operatorname{Var}(X)=E[X^2]-(E[X])^2$.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Substitute">
    $\operatorname{Var}(X)=1.7-(1.1)^2=1.7-1.21=0.49$.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Return to original units">
    $\sigma_X=\sqrt{0.49}=0.7$.
  </Step>
</Steps>

**Final answer.** $\boxed{\operatorname{Var}(X)=0.49,\quad \sigma_X=0.7}$.

</details>

</ExamQuestion>

<ExamQuestion title="Developing: Transform the units">

The daily number $X$ of service calls has $E[X]=12$ and $\operatorname{Var}(X)=9$. A workload score is defined by $Y=2.5X+4$. Find $E[Y]$, $\operatorname{Var}(Y)$, and $\sigma_Y$.

<details>
<summary>Show the complete worked solution</summary>

<Steps>
  <Step title="Transform the mean">
    $E[Y]=2.5(12)+4=34$.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Transform the variance">
    $\operatorname{Var}(Y)=(2.5)^2(9)=56.25$. The $+4$ shift does not affect spread.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Transform the standard deviation">
    $\sigma_Y=|2.5|\sqrt9=2.5(3)=7.5$.
  </Step>
</Steps>

**Final answer.** $\boxed{E[Y]=34, \operatorname{Var}(Y)=56.25, \sigma_Y=7.5}$.

</details>

</ExamQuestion>

<ExamQuestion title="Exam: Find variance from a PMF and then transform">

A game returns $X=-1,2,5$ with probabilities $0.4,0.4,0.2$. Find $E[X]$, $E[X^2]$, and $\operatorname{Var}(X)$. The player’s net score is $Y=-3X+10$. Find $E[Y]$ and $\operatorname{Var}(Y)$.

<details>
<summary>Show the complete worked solution</summary>

<Steps>
  <Step title="Mean">
    $E[X]=(-1)(0.4)+2(0.4)+5(0.2)=1.4$.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Second moment">
    $E[X^2]=1(0.4)+4(0.4)+25(0.2)=7$.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Variance">
    $\operatorname{Var}(X)=7-(1.4)^2=5.04$.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Transform">
    $E[Y]=-3(1.4)+10=5.8$, and $\operatorname{Var}(Y)=(-3)^2(5.04)=45.36$.
  </Step>
</Steps>

**Final answer.** $\boxed{E[X]=1.4, \operatorname{Var}(X)=5.04, E[Y]=5.8, \operatorname{Var}(Y)=45.36}$.

</details>

</ExamQuestion>

## Joint PMFs

The first two questions use this joint PMF. Rows belong to $Y$; columns belong to $X$.

| $Y\backslash X$ | $X=1$ | $X=2$ | $X=3$ | $p_Y(y)$ |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| $Y=0$ | 0.20 | 0.10 | 0.10 | 0.40 |
| $Y=1$ | 0.25 | 0.10 | 0.05 | 0.40 |
| $Y=2$ | 0.10 | 0.05 | 0.05 | 0.20 |
| $p_X(x)$ | 0.55 | 0.25 | 0.20 | 1.00 |

<ExamQuestion title="Foundation: Read a joint table and find marginals">

Use the displayed 3-by-3 joint PMF. Find $P(X=2,Y=1)$, $p_X(1)$, $p_Y(0)$, and verify that the table is valid.

<details>
<summary>Show the complete worked solution</summary>

<Steps>
  <Step title="Read the cell">
    At column $X=2$, row $Y=1$, the cell is $P(X=2,Y=1)=0.10$.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Add the first column">
    $p_X(1)=0.20+0.25+0.10=0.55$.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Add the first row">
    $p_Y(0)=0.20+0.10+0.10=0.40$.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Check validity">
    All nine cells are nonnegative and the row totals give $0.40+0.40+0.20=1$.
  </Step>
</Steps>

**Final answer.** $\boxed{P(2,1)=0.10,\ p_X(1)=0.55,\ p_Y(0)=0.40}$; the joint PMF is valid.

</details>

</ExamQuestion>

<ExamQuestion title="Developing: Condition on a row and a column">

Using the same table, find $P(X=1\mid Y=0)$ and $P(Y=2\mid X=1)$. Explain why the two denominators differ.

<details>
<summary>Show the complete worked solution</summary>

<Steps>
  <Step title="Condition on Y=0">
    The given event is a row with total $p_Y(0)=0.40$, so $P(X=1\mid Y=0)=\frac{0.20}{0.40}=0.5$.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Condition on X=1">
    The given event is now a column with total $p_X(1)=0.55$, so $P(Y=2\mid X=1)=\frac{0.10}{0.55}=\frac{2}{11}\approx0.1818$.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Explain the denominator">
    The denominator is always the probability of what appears after the conditioning bar.
  </Step>
</Steps>

**Final answer.** $\boxed{P(X=1\mid Y=0)=0.5,\quad P(Y=2\mid X=1)=\frac{2}{11}}$.

</details>

</ExamQuestion>

<ExamQuestion title="Exam: Normalise a joint formula and test independence">

Let $p_{X,Y}(x,y)=c(x+y)$ for $x\in\{1,2,3\}$ and $y\in\{1,2\}$. Find $c$, both marginal PMFs, $P(X=3\mid Y=2)$, determine whether $X$ and $Y$ are independent, and calculate $\operatorname{Cov}(X,Y)$.

<details>
<summary>Show the complete worked solution</summary>

<Steps>
  <Step title="Normalise the six weights">
    The weights are $2,3,4$ for $y=1$ and $3,4,5$ for $y=2$; their total is $21$. Hence $c=\frac{1}{21}$.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Find the marginals">
    $p_X(1)=\frac{5}{21},\ p_X(2)=\frac{7}{21},\ p_X(3)=\frac{9}{21}$; and $p_Y(1)=\frac{9}{21},\ p_Y(2)=\frac{12}{21}$.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Condition">
    $P(X=3\mid Y=2)=\frac{\frac{5}{21}}{\frac{12}{21}}=\frac{5}{12}$.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Disprove independence">
    $p(1,1)=\frac{2}{21}$, but $p_X(1)p_Y(1)=\left(\frac{5}{21}\right)\left(\frac{9}{21}\right)=\frac{5}{49}$. The mismatch is enough to conclude dependence.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Calculate covariance">
    $E[X]=\frac{46}{21}$, $E[Y]=\frac{11}{7}$, and $E[XY]=\frac{24}{7}$, so $\operatorname{Cov}(X,Y)=\frac{24}{7}-\left(\frac{46}{21}\right)\left(\frac{11}{7}\right)=-\frac{2}{147}$.
  </Step>
</Steps>

**Final answer.** $\boxed{c=\frac{1}{21},\ P(X=3\mid Y=2)=\frac{5}{12},\ X\not\perp Y,\ \operatorname{Cov}(X,Y)=-\frac{2}{147}}$.

</details>

</ExamQuestion>
