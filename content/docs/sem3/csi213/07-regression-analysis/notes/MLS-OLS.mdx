## 7.1 Method of Least Squares and OLS Derivation

Simple linear regression models the relationship between a dependent variable $Y$ and an independent variable $X$ using a straight line. The goal of Ordinary Least Squares (OLS) is to find the line that minimizes the vertical distances between the observed data points and the predicted values.

<Card>

### Definition 7.1.1 (Simple Linear Regression Model)

For $n$ observed data points $(x_i, y_i)$, the population regression model is:
$$
y_i = \beta_0 + \beta_1 x_i + \epsilon_i
$$
where:
- $\beta_0$ is the population intercept.
- $\beta_1$ is the population slope.
- $\epsilon_i$ is the random error term for the $i$-th observation.

The fitted regression line is $\hat{y}_i = \hat{\beta}_0 + \hat{\beta}_1 x_i$, where $\hat{\beta}_0$ and $\hat{\beta}_1$ are the OLS estimators.

</Card>

<HighlightCard
  variant="neutral"
  title="Theorem 7.1.1: The Objective Function (Sum of Squared Errors)"
  description="OLS finds the parameters $\beta_0$ and $\beta_1$ that minimize the Sum of Squared Errors (SSE), also known as the Residual Sum of Squares (RSS)."
>

$$
SSE(\beta_0, \beta_1) = \sum_{i=1}^n (y_i - \beta_0 - \beta_1 x_i)^2
$$

</HighlightCard>

<StepByStep>
  <StepByStepTitle>Proof: OLS Derivation via Multivariate Calculus</StepByStepTitle>

  <Step number={1}>
    <StepTitle>Set Up the Partial Derivatives</StepTitle>

    To minimize $SSE$, we take the partial derivatives with respect to $\beta_0$ and $\beta_1$ and set them equal to zero (first-order conditions):
    $\frac{\partial SSE}{\partial \beta_0} = -2 \sum_{i=1}^n (y_i - \beta_0 - \beta_1 x_i) = 0$
    $\frac{\partial SSE}{\partial \beta_1} = -2 \sum_{i=1}^n x_i(y_i - \beta_0 - \beta_1 x_i) = 0$
  </Step>

  <Step number={2}>
    <StepTitle>Solve for $\beta_0$</StepTitle>

    From the first normal equation:
    $\sum y_i - n\beta_0 - \beta_1 \sum x_i = 0$
    Divide by $n$:
    $\bar{y} - \beta_0 - \beta_1 \bar{x} = 0$
    Solving for $\beta_0$ yields the intercept estimator:
    $\hat{\beta}_0 = \bar{y} - \hat{\beta}_1 \bar{x}$
  </Step>

  <Step number={3}>
    <StepTitle>Substitute $\beta_0$ into the Second Equation</StepTitle>

    Substitute $\hat{\beta}_0 = \bar{y} - \hat{\beta}_1 \bar{x}$ into the second normal equation:
    $\sum x_i [y_i - (\bar{y} - \hat{\beta}_1 \bar{x}) - \hat{\beta}_1 x_i] = 0$
    $\sum x_i (y_i - \bar{y}) - \hat{\beta}_1 \sum x_i (x_i - \bar{x}) = 0$
  </Step>

  <Step number={4}>
    <StepTitle>Solve for $\beta_1$</StepTitle>

    Isolate $\hat{\beta}_1$:
    $\hat{\beta}_1 = \frac{\sum x_i (y_i - \bar{y})}{\sum x_i (x_i - \bar{x})}$
    Using algebraic identities $\sum (x_i - \bar{x})(y_i - \bar{y}) = \sum x_i y_i - n\bar{x}\bar{y}$ and $\sum (x_i - \bar{x})^2 = \sum x_i^2 - n\bar{x}^2$, we obtain the standard computational formula:
    $\hat{\beta}_1 = \frac{\sum_{i=1}^n (x_i - \bar{x})(y_i - \bar{y})}{\sum_{i=1}^n (x_i - \bar{x})^2} = \frac{n\sum x_i y_i - (\sum x_i)(\sum y_i)}{n\sum x_i^2 - (\sum x_i)^2}$ $\blacksquare$
  </Step>
</StepByStep>

<Card>

### Definition 7.1.2 (OLS Estimators Summary)

The Ordinary Least Squares estimators that minimize the SSE are:

$$
\hat{\beta}_1 = \frac{S_{xy}}{S_{xx}} = \frac{\sum (x_i - \bar{x})(y_i - \bar{y})}{\sum (x_i - \bar{x})^2}
$$
$$
\hat{\beta}_0 = \bar{y} - \hat{\beta}_1 \bar{x}
$$

The regression line always passes through the centroid of the data $(\bar{x}, \bar{y})$.

</Card>
