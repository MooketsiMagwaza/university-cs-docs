## 3.1 Conditional Probability and the Multiplication Rule

In many real-world scenarios, we acquire new information that restricts our sample space. Conditional probability provides the mathematical framework for updating our beliefs about the likelihood of an event given that another event has already occurred.

<Card>

### Definition 3.1.1 (Conditional Probability)

The **Conditional Probability** of an event $B$ given that an event $A$ has occurred, denoted $P(B \mid A)$, is the probability of $B$ within the restricted sample space of $A$. 

Mathematically, it is defined as:
$$
P(B \mid A) = \frac{P(A \cap B)}{P(A)} \quad \text{provided } P(A) > 0
$$

</Card>

<StepByStep>
  <StepByStepTitle>Proof: Complement Rule in Conditional Space</StepByStepTitle>

  <Step number={1}>
    <StepTitle>State the Goal</StepTitle>

    Prove that the conditional probabilities of an event and its complement sum to 1: $P(B \mid A) + P(\overline{B} \mid A) = 1$.
  </Step>

  <Step number={2}>
    <StepTitle>Apply the Definition</StepTitle>

    Substitute the definition of conditional probability for both terms:
    $\frac{P(A \cap B)}{P(A)} + \frac{P(A \cap \overline{B})}{P(A)}$
  </Step>

  <Step number={3}>
    <StepTitle>Combine Numerators</StepTitle>

    Since the denominators are identical, combine the numerators:
    $\frac{P((A \cap B) \cup (A \cap \overline{B}))}{P(A)}$
  </Step>

  <Step number={4}>
    <StepTitle>Apply Set Theory and Conclude</StepTitle>

    By the distributive law, $(A \cap B) \cup (A \cap \overline{B}) = A \cap (B \cup \overline{B}) = A \cap S = A$.
    Therefore, the expression simplifies to $\frac{P(A)}{P(A)} = 1$. $\blacksquare$
  </Step>
</StepByStep>

<HighlightCard
  variant="neutral"
  title="Theorem 3.1.1: The General Multiplication Rule"
  description="Extends the definition of conditional probability to calculate the joint probability of $n$ sequential events."
>

$$
P(A_1 \cap A_2 \cap \dots \cap A_n) = P(A_1) \cdot P(A_2 \mid A_1) \cdot P(A_3 \mid A_1 \cap A_2) \dots P(A_n \mid A_1 \cap \dots \cap A_{n-1})
$$

</HighlightCard>

<StepByStep>
  <StepByStepTitle>Example: Application of the General Multiplication Rule</StepByStepTitle>

  <Step number={1}>
    <StepTitle>Problem Statement</StepTitle>

    A box contains 5 red marbles and 3 blue marbles. Three marbles are drawn sequentially without replacement. What is the probability that all three marbles are red?
  </Step>

  <Step number={2}>
    <StepTitle>Define the Events</StepTitle>

    Let $R_1$ be the event the first marble is red.
    Let $R_2$ be the event the second marble is red.
    Let $R_3$ be the event the third marble is red.
    We need to find $P(R_1 \cap R_2 \cap R_3)$.
  </Step>

  <Step number={3}>
    <StepTitle>Calculate Conditional Probabilities</StepTitle>

    $P(R_1) = \frac{5}{8}$
    $P(R_2 \mid R_1) = \frac{4}{7}$ (4 red remain out of 7 total)
    $P(R_3 \mid R_1 \cap R_2) = \frac{3}{6}$ (3 red remain out of 6 total)
  </Step>

  <Step number={4}>
    <StepTitle>Apply the Multiplication Rule</StepTitle>

    $P(R_1 \cap R_2 \cap R_3) = \frac{5}{8} \times \frac{4}{7} \times \frac{3}{6} = \frac{60}{336} = \frac{5}{28} \approx 0.1786$.
  </Step>
</StepByStep>
