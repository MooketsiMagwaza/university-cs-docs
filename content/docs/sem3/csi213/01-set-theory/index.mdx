# Chapter 1: Set Theory & Combinatorics

The algebraic foundation required to manipulate events, count finite sample spaces, and prove fundamental identities.

---

## Chapter Index

<Card>

### [1.1 Set Theory Definitions](/docs/sem3/csi213/01-set-theory/notes/definitions)
The foundational building blocks of abstract mathematics and logic.

* **Sets & Elements:** Defining collections of objects, membership ($\in$), and set-builder notation.
* **Subsets & Equality:** Rigorous definitions of $A \subseteq B$ and $A = B$.
* **Special Sets:** The Universal Set ($S$ or $U$) and the Empty Set ($\emptyset$).

</Card>

<Card>

### [1.2 Set Algebra](/docs/sem3/csi213/01-set-theory/notes/algebra)
Operations that allow us to combine and manipulate sets to model complex logical conditions.

* **Unions & Intersections:** Combining sets ($A \cup B$) and finding commonalities ($A \cap B$).
* **Complements & Differences:** Defining what is *not* in a set ($A^c$) and relative complements ($A \setminus B$).
* **De Morgan's Laws:** Proving and applying the fundamental duality between unions and intersections under complementation.

</Card>

<Card>

### [1.3 Summation Notation](/docs/sem3/csi213/01-set-theory/notes/summations)
The calculus of discrete mathematics, essential for calculating expected values and probabilities over finite spaces.

* **Sigma ($\Sigma$) Notation:** Formalizing the addition of sequences.
* **Properties of Sums:** Linearity, factoring out constants, and splitting sums.
* **Telescoping & Special Sums:** Arithmetic and geometric series formulas.

</Card>

<Card>

### [1.4 Combinatorics](/docs/sem3/csi213/01-set-theory/notes/combinatorics)
The mathematics of counting, which is the engine behind classical probability.

* **The Multiplication Principle:** Counting sequential independent choices.
* **Permutations:** Counting arrangements where order matters ($P(n, k)$).
* **Combinations:** Counting selections where order does not matter ($\binom{n}{k}$).

</Card>
