## 1.2 Algebra of Sets (Theorems & Proofs)

<HighlightCard
  variant="neutral"
  title="Theorem 1.2.1: Distributive Law 1"
  description="The intersection of a set with the union of two other sets distributes over the union."
>

$$
A \cap (B \cup C) = (A \cap B) \cup (A \cap C)
$$

</HighlightCard>

<StepByStep>
  <StepByStepTitle>Example: Verifying Distributive Law 1</StepByStepTitle>

  <Step number={1}>
    <StepTitle>Define the Sets</StepTitle>

    Let $A = \{1, 2\}$, $B = \{2, 3\}$, $C = \{3, 4\}$.
  </Step>

  <Step number={2}>
    <StepTitle>Evaluate Left-Hand Side (LHS)</StepTitle>

    $B \cup C = \{2, 3, 4\}$
    $A \cap (B \cup C) = \{1, 2\} \cap \{2, 3, 4\} = \{2\}$
  </Step>

  <Step number={3}>
    <StepTitle>Evaluate Right-Hand Side (RHS)</StepTitle>

    $A \cap B = \{2\}$
    $A \cap C = \emptyset$
    $(A \cap B) \cup (A \cap C) = \{2\} \cup \emptyset = \{2\}$

    LHS = RHS, verifying the law.
  </Step>
</StepByStep>

<HighlightCard
  variant="neutral"
  title="Theorem 1.2.2: Distributive Law 2"
  description="The union of a set with the intersection of two other sets distributes over the intersection."
>

$$
A \cup (B \cap C) = (A \cup B) \cap (A \cup C)
$$

</HighlightCard>

<StepByStep>
  <StepByStepTitle>Example: Verifying Distributive Law 2</StepByStepTitle>

  <Step number={1}>
    <StepTitle>Define the Sets</StepTitle>

    Let $A = \{1\}$, $B = \{1, 2\}$, $C = \{2, 3\}$.
  </Step>

  <Step number={2}>
    <StepTitle>Evaluate LHS</StepTitle>

    $B \cap C = \{2\}$
    $A \cup (B \cap C) = \{1\} \cup \{2\} = \{1, 2\}$
  </Step>

  <Step number={3}>
    <StepTitle>Evaluate RHS</StepTitle>

    $A \cup B = \{1, 2\}$
    $A \cup C = \{1, 2, 3\}$
    $(A \cup B) \cap (A \cup C) = \{1, 2\} \cap \{1, 2, 3\} = \{1, 2\}$

    LHS = RHS, verifying the law.
  </Step>
</StepByStep>

<HighlightCard variant="neutral" title="Theorem 1.2.3: De Morgan's Law for Union" description="The complement of a union is the intersection of the complements.">

$$
\overline{A \cup B} = \overline{A} \cap \overline{B}
$$

</HighlightCard>

<StepByStep>
  <StepByStepTitle>Proof: De Morgan's Law for Union via Double-Inclusion</StepByStepTitle>
  <Step number={1}>
    <StepTitle>Goal</StepTitle>
    Prove $\overline{A \cup B} = \overline{A} \cap \overline{B}$ by showing $\overline{A \cup B} \subseteq \overline{A} \cap \overline{B}$ and $\overline{A} \cap \overline{B} \subseteq \overline{A \cup B}$.
  </Step>
  <Step number={2}>
    <StepTitle>Part 1: Forward Inclusion ($\subseteq$)</StepTitle>
    Let $x \in \overline{A \cup B}$. <br/>
    By definition of complement, $x \notin (A \cup B)$. <br/>
    This means $x$ is neither in $A$ nor in $B$. Therefore, $x \notin A$ AND $x \notin B$. <br/>
    By definition of complement, $x \in \overline{A}$ and $x \in \overline{B}$. <br/>
    Thus, $x \in \overline{A} \cap \overline{B}$. $\blacksquare$
  </Step>
  <Step number={3}>
    <StepTitle>Part 2: Reverse Inclusion ($\supseteq$)</StepTitle>
    Let $x \in \overline{A} \cap \overline{B}$. <br/>
    By definition of intersection, $x \in \overline{A}$ and $x \in \overline{B}$. <br/>
    This means $x \notin A$ and $x \notin B$. <br/>
    Therefore, $x$ cannot be in $A \cup B$, meaning $x \notin (A \cup B)$. <br/>
    By definition of complement, $x \in \overline{A \cup B}$. $\blacksquare$
  </Step>
  <Step number={4}>
    <StepTitle>Conclusion</StepTitle>
    Since both subset relations hold, $\overline{A \cup B} = \overline{A} \cap \overline{B}$.
  </Step>
</StepByStep>

<HighlightCard variant="neutral" title="Theorem 1.2.4: De Morgan's Law for Intersection" description="The complement of an intersection is the union of the complements.">

$$
\overline{A \cap B} = \overline{A} \cup \overline{B}
$$

</HighlightCard>

<StepByStep>
  <StepByStepTitle>Proof: De Morgan's Law for Intersection via Double-Inclusion</StepByStepTitle>
  <Step number={1}>
    <StepTitle>Goal</StepTitle>
    Prove $\overline{A \cap B} = \overline{A} \cup \overline{B}$ by showing $\overline{A \cap B} \subseteq \overline{A} \cup \overline{B}$ and $\overline{A} \cup \overline{B} \subseteq \overline{A \cap B}$.
  </Step>
  <Step number={2}>
    <StepTitle>Part 1: Forward Inclusion ($\subseteq$)</StepTitle>
    Let $x \in \overline{A \cap B}$. <br/>
    By definition of complement, $x \notin (A \cap B)$. <br/>
    This means it is not true that $x$ is in both $A$ and $B$. Therefore, $x \notin A$ OR $x \notin B$. <br/>
    By definition of complement, $x \in \overline{A}$ OR $x \in \overline{B}$. <br/>
    Thus, $x \in \overline{A} \cup \overline{B}$. $\blacksquare$
  </Step>
  <Step number={3}>
    <StepTitle>Part 2: Reverse Inclusion ($\supseteq$)</StepTitle>
    Let $x \in \overline{A} \cup \overline{B}$. <br/>
    By definition of union, $x \in \overline{A}$ OR $x \in \overline{B}$. <br/>
    This means $x \notin A$ OR $x \notin B$. <br/>
    Therefore, $x$ cannot be in both $A$ and $B$, meaning $x \notin (A \cap B)$. <br/>
    By definition of complement, $x \in \overline{A \cap B}$. $\blacksquare$
  </Step>
  <Step number={4}>
    <StepTitle>Conclusion</StepTitle>
    Since both subset relations hold, $\overline{A \cap B} = \overline{A} \cup \overline{B}$.
  </Step>
</StepByStep>

<HighlightCard variant="neutral" title="Theorem 1.2.5: Identity Laws" description="Sets combined with the empty set or universal set retain their original identity.">

$$
A \cup \emptyset = A \quad \text{and} \quad A \cap U = A
$$

</HighlightCard>

<HighlightCard variant="neutral" title="Theorem 1.2.6: Domination Laws" description="Sets combined with the universal set or empty set are dominated by the extreme set.">

$$
A \cup U = U \quad \text{and} \quad A \cap \emptyset = \emptyset
$$

</HighlightCard>

<HighlightCard variant="neutral" title="Theorem 1.2.7: Idempotent Laws" description="Combining a set with itself yields the same set.">

$$
A \cup A = A \quad \text{and} \quad A \cap A = A
$$

</HighlightCard>

<HighlightCard variant="neutral" title="Theorem 1.2.8: Complement Laws" description="A set combined with its complement yields the universal set or empty set.">

$$
A \cup \overline{A} = U \quad \text{and} \quad A \cap \overline{A} = \emptyset
$$

</HighlightCard>

<HighlightCard variant="neutral" title="Theorem 1.2.9: Commutative Laws" description="The order of sets in union or intersection does not matter.">

$$
A \cup B = B \cup A \quad \text{and} \quad A \cap B = B \cap A
$$

</HighlightCard>

<HighlightCard variant="neutral" title="Theorem 1.2.10: Associative Laws" description="Grouping of sets in union or intersection does not affect the result.">

$$
(A \cup B) \cup C = A \cup (B \cup C) \quad \text{and} \quad (A \cap B) \cap C = A \cap (B \cap C)
$$

</HighlightCard>

<HighlightCard variant="neutral" title="Theorem 1.2.11: Absorption Laws" description="A set absorbs the intersection or union with a superset/subset combination.">

$$
A \cup (A \cap B) = A \quad \text{and} \quad A \cap (A \cup B) = A
$$

</HighlightCard>

<StepByStep>
  <StepByStepTitle>Example: Proving Absorption Law ($A \cup (A \cap B) = A$)</StepByStepTitle>

  <Step number={1}>
    <StepTitle>Start with the Left-Hand Side</StepTitle>

    $A \cup (A \cap B)$
  </Step>

  <Step number={2}>
    <StepTitle>Apply Identity Law ($A = A \cap U$)</StepTitle>

    $(A \cap U) \cup (A \cap B)$
  </Step>

  <Step number={3}>
    <StepTitle>Apply Distributive Law (Factor out A)</StepTitle>

    $A \cap (U \cup B)$
  </Step>

  <Step number={4}>
    <StepTitle>Apply Domination Law ($U \cup B = U$) and Identity Law</StepTitle>

    $A \cap U = A$ $\blacksquare$
  </Step>
</StepByStep>

<StepByStep>
  <StepByStepTitle>Proof: Set Difference Associativity ($(A - B) - C = A - (B \cup C)$)</StepByStepTitle>

  <Step number={1}>
    <StepTitle>Start with the definition of set difference</StepTitle>

    $(A - B) - C = (A \cap \overline{B}) \cap \overline{C}$
  </Step>

  <Step number={2}>
    <StepTitle>Apply associativity of intersection</StepTitle>

    $A \cap (\overline{B} \cap \overline{C})$
  </Step>

  <Step number={3}>
    <StepTitle>Apply De Morgan's Law</StepTitle>

    $A \cap \overline{(B \cup C)}$
  </Step>

  <Step number={4}>
    <StepTitle>Convert back to set difference</StepTitle>

    $A - (B \cup C)$ $\blacksquare$
  </Step>
</StepByStep>

<StepByStep>
  <StepByStepTitle>Proof: De Morgan's on Set Difference ($A - (B \cup C) = (A - B) \cap (A - C)$)</StepByStepTitle>

  <Step number={1}>
    <StepTitle>Apply definition of set difference</StepTitle>

    $A - (B \cup C) = A \cap \overline{(B \cup C)}$
  </Step>

  <Step number={2}>
    <StepTitle>Apply De Morgan's Law to the complement</StepTitle>

    $A \cap (\overline{B} \cap \overline{C})$
  </Step>

  <Step number={3}>
    <StepTitle>Rearrange using associativity and commutativity</StepTitle>

    $(A \cap \overline{B}) \cap (A \cap \overline{C})$
  </Step>

  <Step number={4}>
    <StepTitle>Convert back to set difference</StepTitle>

    $(A - B) \cap (A - C)$ $\blacksquare$
  </Step>
</StepByStep>
