# Chapter 2: Axiomatic Probability

The measure-theoretic foundation of probability. This chapter establishes the rigorous mathematical framework for defining sample spaces, events, and the unbreakable axioms that govern all probabilistic calculations.

---

## Chapter Index

<Card>

### [2.1 Sample Spaces & Events](/docs/sem3/csi213/02-probability/notes/sample-space-events)
The foundational structures of probability theory, mapping real-world experiments to abstract mathematical spaces.

* **Sample Spaces ($S$):** Defining the universal set of all possible outcomes for discrete and continuous experiments.
* **Events as Subsets:** Rigorously defining events as subsets of $S$ ($A \subseteq S$) and applying set theory operations.
* **Mutually Exclusive & Exhaustive Events:** Defining disjoint events and partitions of the sample space.

### [2.2 Outcomes & Classical Probability](/docs/sem3/csi213/02-probability/notes/outcomes)
Bridging abstract sets with concrete outcomes and the historical definition of probability.

* **Elementary Outcomes:** Analyzing individual outcomes and their role in finite sample spaces.
* **Classical Probability:** Calculating probabilities using equally likely outcomes ($P(A) = |A| / |S|$).
* **Partitions:** Breaking down complex events into simpler, non-overlapping outcome sets to simplify calculations.

<Card>

### [2.3 The Kolmogorov Axioms](/docs/sem3/csi213/02-probability/notes/axioms)
The absolute source of truth for modern probability, grounded in measure theory.

* **The Three Axioms:** Explicitly stating Axiom I (Non-negativity), Axiom II (Normalization), and Axiom III (Countable Additivity).
* **Theorem (Addition Rule):** Proving $P(A \cup B) = P(A) + P(B) - P(A \cap B)$ by partitioning $A \cup B = (A \cap B^c) \cup B$ and applying Axiom III.
* **Theorem (Monotonicity):** Proving that if $A \subset B$, then $P(A) \le P(B)$ using the partition $B = A \cup (B \cap A^c)$.

</Card>
