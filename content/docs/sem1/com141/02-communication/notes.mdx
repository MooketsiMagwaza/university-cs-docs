## Communication

Communication is the purposeful exchange of meaning. It is not complete merely because a sender has transmitted words; the receiver must interpret the message, and feedback helps confirm or correct that interpretation.

## The communication process

The basic model contains a sender, a message, encoding, a channel, a receiver, decoding, feedback, and context. Noise can affect any stage. Noise includes sound, poor internet, unfamiliar terminology, assumptions, stress, and information overload.

| Element | Example in a group project |
| --- | --- |
| Sender | A member proposing a plan |
| Message | The proposed tasks and deadline |
| Channel | Meeting, chat, or shared document |
| Receiver | The rest of the group |
| Feedback | Questions, agreement, or a revised plan |
| Context | The assignment brief and available time |

## Verbal, non-verbal, and written modes

Verbal communication includes word choice, structure, pace, and tone. Non-verbal communication includes posture, facial expression, eye contact, gestures, and distance. Written communication gives the reader time to inspect the message, but removes many immediate cues, so headings, paragraphing, and explicit requests matter.

Choose a channel for the task. A quick clarification may suit chat; a complicated disagreement may need a meeting; a final decision should be recorded in a shared document or email.

## Barriers and repair

Common barriers are ambiguous language, jargon, cultural assumptions, poor timing, selective attention, and defensive responses. Repair a misunderstanding by restating the message, asking an open question, and checking the interpretation: “I understood the deadline as Tuesday at 10:00. Is that what you meant?”

Academic communication values evidence, careful claims, and respect for alternative views. Separate observation from interpretation and interpretation from conclusion. Disagree with the idea rather than the person, and support a claim with a source or clearly labelled example.

## Checklist

- Who needs this message?
- What action or understanding should result?
- Is the channel appropriate?
- Could a term or assumption be misunderstood?
- How will the receiver confirm completion?
