---
title: "Information Literacy and Source Evaluation"
description: "Turning a topic into a search, choosing and evaluating sources, and keeping a research record that respects copyright and attribution."
---

Information literacy is the ability to recognise an information need, find suitable sources, evaluate them, use them ethically and communicate the result. Searching is only one step in that chain. This topic shows how to turn a question into a search, how to judge what you find, and how to record it so that your work can be traced back to its sources.

<LearningObjectives items={[
  "Turn a topic into a search with key concepts, synonyms, Boolean operators (AND, OR, NOT) and phrase searching.",
  "Choose between the library catalogue, research databases and a general web search for a given need.",
  "Classify a source as primary, secondary or tertiary, and match it to the claim it can support.",
  "Evaluate a source for authority, accuracy, currency, relevance and purpose.",
  "Keep a research record with full bibliographic details, the key claim and your own evaluation for each source.",
  "Explain why copyright, database licences and attribution apply to ideas, images, data and code as well as to sentences.",
]} />
