## Listening and Speaking

Listening is an active process: attend to the message, interpret it, connect it to prior knowledge, and respond appropriately. Hearing sound without processing its meaning is not effective academic listening.

## Active listening and note-making

Before a lecture, preview the topic and write two questions. During the lecture, listen for definitions, examples, contrasts, reasons, and transitions such as “therefore” or “in contrast.” Afterward, close the slides and write the main idea from memory, then revisit gaps.

The Cornell method divides a page into notes, cues, and a summary. The outline method shows hierarchy through indentation. A concept map is useful when ideas have many relationships. Distinguish the lecturer’s claim from your own question and from a source quotation.

Good notes are selective. Capture structure and reasoning, not every sentence. Leave space for questions and add the date, course, topic, and source at the top.

## Planning a presentation

Define the audience and purpose first. A simple structure is an opening with the topic and roadmap, a body with two to four main points supported by examples or evidence, and a close that answers the question and states the takeaway.

Slides should support speech, not become a script. Use readable text, meaningful diagrams, and consistent labels. Rehearse aloud, time the presentation, and prepare a shorter version in case the schedule changes.

## Delivery and questions

Speak slightly slower than ordinary conversation, pause after important ideas, and look at the audience rather than the screen. If you do not know an answer, say what you can establish and offer to verify the remaining point. A question is an opportunity to clarify the audience’s needs, not a challenge to win.

## Common mistakes

- Reading slides word for word.
- Using dense paragraphs or unexplained graphics.
- Giving examples without stating the point they demonstrate.
- Failing to cite images, data, or borrowed ideas.
- Ending without answering the stated purpose.
