---
title: "Reading for Academic Purposes"
description: "Purposeful reading: previewing, skimming, scanning, close and critical reading, and reading figures and equations."
---

Academic reading is purposeful: a textbook definition, a research article and an assignment brief each need to be read differently, because each asks for different evidence. This topic gives you a sequence of passes over a text, a way to find the main idea of a paragraph, and critical questions to put to an author. Every later task in the course, from searching to writing, begins with reading well.

<LearningObjectives items={[
  "Preview a text using its title, abstract or introduction, headings, figures, conclusion and reference list.",
  "Distinguish skimming, scanning, close reading and critical reading, and choose the right one for a purpose.",
  "Apply the SQ3R pattern (Survey, Question, Read, Recite, Review) to a long chapter.",
  "Identify the controlling idea of a paragraph and the job it does: defining, contrasting, giving evidence or concluding.",
  "Interpret a figure or an equation by reading its caption, axes, units, legend, source and variables before drawing a conclusion.",
  "Ask critical questions about assumptions, evidence and limitations, and annotate selectively instead of highlighting everything.",
]} />
