## Polynomial and Rational Functions

A polynomial has the form $a_nx^n+\cdots+a_1x+a_0$ with non-negative integer exponents. Its degree and leading coefficient describe end behaviour.

## Factor and remainder theorems

When $p(x)$ is divided by $x-a$, the remainder is $p(a)$. Therefore $p(a)=0$ exactly when $(x-a)$ is a factor. Use this to test candidate roots and reduce the degree by division.

## Rational functions

A rational function is $f(x)=p(x)/q(x)$ with $q(x)\ne0$. Zeros of the denominator are excluded from the domain. A factor that cancels may create a removable hole; an uncancelled denominator zero typically produces a vertical asymptote.

Horizontal asymptotes come from comparing degrees and leading coefficients. For equal degrees, the asymptote is the ratio of leading coefficients; if the denominator degree is larger, the horizontal asymptote is $y=0$.

## Worked example

For
$$
f(x)=\frac{x^2-4}{x-2}=\frac{(x-2)(x+2)}{x-2},
$$
the simplified rule is $x+2$, but $x=2$ remains excluded. The graph is a line with a hole at $(2,4)$, not a complete line.

## Common mistakes

- Treating a cancelled denominator factor as if it restored the excluded input.
- Forgetting multiplicity when interpreting roots.
- Using the factor theorem without checking the exact divisor $x-a$.
- Drawing asymptotes as if the function must touch them.
