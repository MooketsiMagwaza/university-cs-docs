## Complex Numbers

A complex number has the form $z=a+bi$, where $a,b\in\mathbb R$ and $i^2=-1$. The real part is $a$ and the imaginary part is $b$.

## Arithmetic and conjugates

Add and subtract componentwise. Multiply using $i^2=-1$. The conjugate of $z=a+bi$ is $\bar z=a-bi$, and
$$
z\bar z=a^2+b^2=|z|^2.
$$
To divide, multiply numerator and denominator by the denominator's conjugate.

## Argand and polar form

Plot $a+bi$ as $(a,b)$. The modulus is $r=\sqrt{a^2+b^2}$ and an argument $\theta$ satisfies $\tan\theta=b/a$ with the correct quadrant. Then
$$
z=r(\cos\theta+i\sin\theta).
$$

## De Moivre's theorem

$$
[r(\cos\theta+i\sin\theta)]^n=r^n(\cos n\theta+i\sin n\theta).
$$
$$
\text{cis}(\theta)=\cos\theta+i\sin\theta
$$
is a convenient shorthand for polar calculations.

## Roots

If $z=r\,\text{cis}(\theta)$, its $n$ distinct roots are
$$
w_k=\sqrt[n]{r}\,\text{cis}\left(\frac{\theta+2\pi k}{n}\right),\qquad k=0,1,\ldots,n-1.
$$
They are equally spaced around a circle in the Argand plane.

## Common mistakes

- Losing the quadrant when finding an argument.
- Listing only one root.
- Treating $i^2$ as $1$.
- Forgetting that arguments differ by multiples of $2\pi$.
