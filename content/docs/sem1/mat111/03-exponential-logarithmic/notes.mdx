## Exponential and Logarithmic Functions

For $a>0$, $a\ne1$, the exponential function $a^x$ has domain $\mathbb R$, range $(0,\infty)$, and inverse logarithm $\log_a x$.

## Laws

$$
a^ma^n=a^{m+n},\quad \frac{a^m}{a^n}=a^{m-n},\quad (a^m)^n=a^{mn}
$$
and
$$
\log_a(xy)=\log_a x+\log_a y,\quad
\log_a(x/y)=\log_a x-\log_a y.
$$

The logarithm laws require positive arguments. Never split $\log(a+b)$ into a sum; there is no such product law for addition.

## Solving equations

If the bases match, equate exponents. Otherwise take logarithms of both sides. For logarithmic equations, state the domain first and reject any answer that makes an original log argument non-positive.

## Graphs

Exponential growth has a horizontal asymptote and its inverse logarithm has a vertical asymptote. Translations inside the exponent affect horizontal position; translations outside affect vertical position.

## Visualise it

Move $d$ and the horizontal asymptote of $2^x$ moves with the graph. Move $c$ and the graph slides sideways, but the asymptote stays at $y=d$. Then pick $\ln x$ and watch its vertical asymptote follow $c$.

## Worked example

Solve $2^{x+1}=16$. Since $16=2^4$, $x+1=4$ and $x=3$. If the equation were $2^x=7$, use $x=\log_2 7=\ln7/\ln2$.

## Common mistakes

- Applying logarithm laws to a sum.
- Accepting a solution that violates a log domain.
- Confusing the base with the argument.
- Forgetting that exponential functions are positive for every real input.
