## Trigonometry

The unit circle defines sine and cosine for every real angle. Their period is $2\pi$, while tangent has period $\pi$ because $\tan x=\sin x/\cos x$.

## Core identities

$$
\sin^2x+\cos^2x=1,\qquad
1+\tan^2x=\sec^2x.
$$

Sum and difference formulas include
$$
\sin(a\pm b)=\sin a\cos b\pm\cos a\sin b,
$$
and the double-angle identities follow by setting $a=b$.

## Sine and cosine rules

In a triangle, $a/\sin A=b/\sin B=c/\sin C$. The cosine rule is $c^2=a^2+b^2-2ab\cos C$. Draw the triangle and match side labels to opposite angles before substituting.

## Equations and extrema

Solve within the stated interval, then use periodicity to find all solutions. For $y=R\sin(x-\phi)+D$, the amplitude is $|R|$, the midline is $y=D$, and the range is $[D-|R|,D+|R|]$.

## Worked example

For $2\sin x=1$ on $[0,2\pi]$, $\sin x=1/2$, so $x=\pi/6$ or $5\pi/6$. The interval is essential; the general solution contains infinitely many values.

## Common mistakes

- Mixing degrees and radians.
- Dividing by a trigonometric expression that might be zero.
- Giving only one solution when the interval contains several.
- Using the sine rule without checking the ambiguous case.
