---
title: "Proof Techniques"
description: "What a proof is, and how to write direct proofs and proofs by contraposition, contradiction, cases and induction."
---

A proof is a logically connected explanation that starts from accepted definitions, hypotheses and theorems and arrives at a stated conclusion; it is not a list of examples. This topic introduces the standard techniques, from direct proof to induction, and the habits that make a proof complete and checkable. They are the tools the course is designed to build, since its aim is to let you read and construct rigorous proofs.

<LearningObjectives items={[
  "Write a direct proof of an implication by assuming the hypothesis and working from definitions such as even and odd integers.",
  "Prove an implication through its contrapositive, and explain when that is easier than a direct proof.",
  "Write a proof by contradiction that states exactly where the contradiction occurs.",
  "Split a claim into exhaustive cases, such as even and odd integers or non-negative and negative numbers, and prove each case.",
  "Prove a statement for all integers from a starting value by induction, setting out the base case, hypothesis, step and conclusion, and use strong induction when the next case depends on several earlier ones.",
  "Check a proof against a checklist: the domain and hypotheses are stated, definitions are used, every variable is introduced, and both directions of an if and only if are proved.",
]} />
