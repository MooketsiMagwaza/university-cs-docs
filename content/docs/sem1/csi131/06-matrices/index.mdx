---
title: "Integers and Matrices"
description: "Divisibility of integers, matrix notation and operations, and zero-one matrices for representing relations and graphs."
---

Discrete mathematics works with integers because many objects in computer science are counted, indexed or stored as finite bit patterns. This topic introduces divisibility and the rules for matrix equality, addition and multiplication. It then uses zero-one matrices to represent relations and graph edges, so that a matrix can be read as a model and not just a grid of numbers.

<LearningObjectives items={[
  "Use integer notation, and say when one integer divides another, including that divisibility is transitive.",
  "Describe a matrix by its dimensions, and decide when equality, addition and multiplication are defined.",
  "Multiply two matrices with the row-column rule, and compute a chosen entry of the product.",
  "Explain why matrix multiplication is generally not commutative.",
  "Interpret a zero-one matrix as a relation or as the adjacency matrix of a graph, and say what a 1 in a given position means.",
]} />
