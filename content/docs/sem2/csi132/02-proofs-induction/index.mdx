---
title: "Proofs and Induction"
description: "Choosing a proof strategy, proving and disproving quantified statements, and using induction, strong induction and counterexamples."
---

A proof starts by identifying the hypothesis and the exact conclusion, then expanding the relevant definitions before a strategy is chosen. This topic covers direct proof, the contrapositive, contradiction, cases, counterexamples, and ordinary and strong induction, with attention to how quantifiers and their order change what is claimed. Induction is used again in the next topic to verify formulas.

<LearningObjectives items={[
  "Begin a proof by identifying the hypothesis and the exact conclusion, and expand the definitions involved.",
  "Choose between direct proof, the contrapositive, contradiction and proof by cases, and show that the cases are exhaustive.",
  "Disprove a universal statement with a single counterexample, and explain why an example cannot prove a universal claim.",
  "Prove a statement for all integers from a starting value by induction, with a base case, hypothesis, step and quantified conclusion.",
  "Use strong induction when a new object is built from several smaller ones.",
  "Read and write quantified statements correctly, recognising that the order of quantifiers changes the meaning.",
]} />
