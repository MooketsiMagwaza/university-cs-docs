## Sequences and Summations

A sequence is a function from a set of integers, usually $\mathbb N$, into a target set. The notation $(a_n)$ describes the term indexed by $n$; it does not mean that the terms form a set, since order and repetition matter.

## Common forms

An arithmetic sequence has constant difference $d$: $a_n=a_1+(n-1)d$. A geometric sequence has constant ratio $r$: $a_n=a_1r^{n-1}$. Their finite sums are

$$\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_i=\frac n2(2a_1+(n-1)d),\qquad \sum_{i=0}^{n-1}ar^i=a\frac{1-r^n}{1-r}\quad(r\ne1).$$

Sigma notation is manipulated by splitting sums, pulling out constants, and shifting indices carefully. For example, $\sum_{i=1}^n(ca_i+db_i)=c\sum_{i=1}^na_i+d\sum_{i=1}^nb_i$.

## Recurrences and recursion

A recurrence defines a term using earlier terms. Initial conditions are part of the definition. For $a_n=a_{n-1}+d$, repeated expansion gives the arithmetic formula. For $a_n=ra_{n-1}$, expansion gives $a_n=r^{n-1}a_1$. Verification by induction is essential.

A recursive algorithm needs a base case and a step that moves toward it. For factorial, $0!=1$ is the base and $n!=n(n-1)!$ is the step. Trace both the calls and the returns; the value is produced while recursion unwinds.

## Common mistakes

- Mixing zero-based and one-based indices.
- Dropping a boundary term while shifting a sum.
- Using the finite geometric formula when $r=1$ without handling that case.
- Giving a recurrence without initial values.
- Assuming a pattern continues without proof.
