---
title: "Discrete Probability Distributions"
description: "Choosing and using the Bernoulli, binomial, geometric and Poisson distributions by matching the structure of the experiment."
---

A distribution should be chosen by matching its experiment, not by recognising a keyword: identify the trial, what counts as a success, how many trials there are, whether sampling replaces items, and whether the trials are independent. This topic works through the Bernoulli, binomial, geometric and Poisson models with their probabilities, means and variances. It also shows how a convention, such as counting trials or counting failures for the geometric distribution, changes the formulas.

<LearningObjectives items={[

  "Use the Poisson distribution for a count of events in a fixed interval, and explain why its rate is not a probability.",
  "Explain when a binomial model is not appropriate because the trials are dependent.",
