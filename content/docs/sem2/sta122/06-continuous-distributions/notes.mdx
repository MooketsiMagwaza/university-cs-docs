## Continuous Probability Distributions

A continuous random variable is described by a probability density function (pdf) $f(x)$ with $f(x)\ge0$ and $\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}f(x)dx=1$. Probabilities are areas:

$$P(a\le X\le b)=\int_a^b f(x)dx.$$

For a continuous variable, $P(X=a)=0$, so endpoints do not change interval probabilities.

## Uniform distribution

For $X\sim U(a,b)$, the density is $1/(b-a)$ on $[a,b]$, and $P(c\le X\le d)=(d-c)/(b-a)$ for an interval inside the support. The mean is $(a+b)/2$ and variance is $(b-a)^2/12$.

## Normal distribution

The normal distribution is symmetric around its mean $\mu$ with standard deviation $\sigma$. Standardise with $Z=(X-\mu)/\sigma$, then use the standard normal cumulative distribution function. The empirical 68-95-99.7 rule gives approximate proportions within one, two, and three standard deviations for a normal model.

## Density versus probability

The height $f(x)$ is not itself a probability and can exceed 1 when the support is narrow. Probability is the area over an interval. The CDF $F(x)=P(X\le x)$ is obtained by integrating the pdf from the left.

## Explore it

Pick the normal or the uniform distribution and shade an interval. For the normal, shade from about $\mu-\sigma$ to $\mu+\sigma$ and read a probability near 0.68.

## Common mistakes

- Assigning positive probability to one exact continuous value.
- Forgetting to standardise with the correct $\sigma$.
- Using a pdf height as an interval probability.
- Ignoring the support where the density is zero.
