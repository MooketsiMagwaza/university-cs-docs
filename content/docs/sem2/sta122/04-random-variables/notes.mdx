## Random Variables

A random variable $X$ assigns a numerical value to each outcome. A discrete random variable has countable possible values and a probability mass function (pmf) $p(x)=P(X=x)$. The pmf must satisfy $p(x)\ge0$ and $\sum_xp(x)=1$.

The cumulative distribution function is $F(x)=P(X\le x)$. It is non-decreasing, approaches 0 on the far left, and approaches 1 on the far right. For a discrete variable, jumps in $F$ equal point probabilities.

## Mean and variance

The expected value is $E[X]=\sum_x xp(x)$. Variance measures spread:

$$\operatorname{Var}(X)=E[(X-\mu)^2]=E[X^2]-\mu^2.$$

For constants $a,b$, $E[aX+b]=aE[X]+b$ and $\operatorname{Var}(aX+b)=a^2\operatorname{Var}(X)$. Adding a constant shifts the distribution but does not change its spread.

## Modelling

Define what one trial means and what $X$ counts. “Number of successes” differs from “trial on which the first success occurs.” Check the support, total probability, and whether outcomes are independent before selecting a distribution.

## Explore it

The expected value is the long-run average. Roll a die many times and watch the running mean settle at $E[X]=3.5$.

## Common mistakes

- Forgetting to include every support value.
- Confusing standard deviation with variance.
- Applying linearity of variance as though it were linear without independence or covariance information.
- Treating a random variable as the same thing as an outcome.
