---
title: "Probability Foundations"
description: "The probability axioms, the complement and addition rules, probabilities found by counting, and checks for whether an answer is reasonable."
---

A probability measure assigns a number from 0 to 1 to each event, following a small set of axioms. This topic develops the complement and addition rules, probability by counting with and without replacement, and a set of reasonableness checks that catch most mistakes. These rules are the base for conditional probability and for the distributions that follow.

<LearningObjectives items={[
  "State the axioms of probability, and find the probability of an event as the ratio of favourable to total outcomes when outcomes are equally likely.",
  "Apply the complement rule, including for questions that ask for at least one success.",
  "Apply the addition rule to any two events and to disjoint events.",
  "Count outcomes with the product rule and combinations, deciding whether sampling is with or without replacement and whether order matters.",
  "Check an answer for reasonableness: it lies between 0 and 1, and a union cannot exceed the sum of its parts.",
  "Compare a relative frequency from a simulation with the probability found by counting.",
]} />

  "Counting with the product rule and combinations (the notes use these without deriving them)",
]} />
