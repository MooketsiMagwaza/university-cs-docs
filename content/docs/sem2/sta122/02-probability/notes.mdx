## Probability Foundations

A probability measure assigns numbers from 0 to 1 to events. The axioms are $P(\Omega)=1$, $P(A)\ge0$, and countable additivity for disjoint events. In a finite equally likely sample space, $P(A)=|A|/|\Omega|$.

## Core rules

The complement rule is $P(A^c)=1-P(A)$. For any events,

$$P(A\cup B)=P(A)+P(B)-P(A\cap B).$$

If $A$ and $B$ are disjoint, the intersection is zero and probabilities add. For “at least one” questions, the complement is often simpler: $P(\text{at least one success})=1-P(\text{no successes})$.

## Counting probability

Use the product rule for sequential choices and combinations when order does not matter. State whether sampling is with or without replacement; that decision changes both the sample space and the probability.

## Reasonableness checks

Every probability must lie in $[0,1]$. The probability of a union cannot exceed the sum of its parts. If two events are disjoint, their total cannot exceed 1. Estimate the direction of an answer before calculating so a misplaced complement is easier to detect.

## Common mistakes

- Adding overlapping events without subtracting the intersection.
- Using a combination when order is part of an outcome.
- Treating dependent draws as independent.
- Reporting a percentage as a decimal without stating the form.
