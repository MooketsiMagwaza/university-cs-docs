## Coordinate Geometry

The slope of a non-vertical line through $(x_1,y_1)$ and $(x_2,y_2)$ is $m=(y_2-y_1)/(x_2-x_1)$. Point-slope form is $y-y_1=m(x-x_1)$, and slope-intercept form is $y=mx+c$.

Parallel non-vertical lines have equal slopes. Perpendicular lines have slopes whose product is $-1$ when both slopes are defined. A vertical line has equation $x=a$ and a horizontal line has equation $y=b$.

## Distance and midpoint

The distance between points is $d=\sqrt{(x_2-x_1)^2+(y_2-y_1)^2}$ and the midpoint is $((x_1+x_2)/2,(y_1+y_2)/2)$. These formulas follow from the right triangle formed by horizontal and vertical changes.

## Circles and conics

A circle with centre $(h,k)$ and radius $r$ has $(x-h)^2+(y-k)^2=r^2$. Complete the square to convert a quadratic equation into this form. For a parabola, identify the axis and vertex before interpreting the focus or directrix; for an ellipse or hyperbola, inspect the squared terms and signs.

## Intersections

To find where two curves meet, solve their equations simultaneously. The number of real solutions corresponds to the number of intersection points. Check candidates in both original equations and state restrictions such as denominators not being zero.

## Common mistakes

- Treating a vertical line as having slope zero.
- Using the negative reciprocal rule without checking for horizontal or vertical lines.
- Losing a sign while completing the square.
- Reporting algebraic solutions that violate the original domain.
