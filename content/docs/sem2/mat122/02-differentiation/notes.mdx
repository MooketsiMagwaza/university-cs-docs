## Differentiation

The derivative $f'(a)$ is the instantaneous rate of change at $a$ and the slope of the tangent line. From first principles,

$$f'(a)=\lim_{h\to0}\frac{f(a+h)-f(a)}h.$$

For $f(x)=x^2$, expansion gives $[(a+h)^2-a^2]/h=2a+h$, whose limit is $2a$.

## Rules

The power rule is $\frac d{dx}x^n=nx^{n-1}$. Use linearity for sums and constants, the product rule $(fg)'=f'g+fg'$, and the quotient rule $(f/g)'=(f'g-fg')/g^2$. The chain rule differentiates a composition: $\frac d{dx}f(g(x))=f'(g(x))g'(x)$.

Standard derivatives include $(e^x)'=e^x$, $(\ln x)'=1/x$, $(\sin x)'=\cos x$, and $(\cos x)'=-\sin x$. Apply the chain rule to functions such as $\ln(3x+1)$.

## Implicit differentiation

When $y$ is defined indirectly by an equation, differentiate both sides with respect to $x$ and treat $y$ as a function of $x$. Thus $d(y^2)/dx=2y\,dy/dx$. Collect derivative terms and solve for $dy/dx$.

## Tangents and rates

At $x=a$, the tangent line is $y-f(a)=f'(a)(x-a)$. In a related-rates problem, write an equation linking quantities, differentiate with respect to time, substitute known values only after differentiating, and check units.

## Common mistakes

- Forgetting the inner derivative in a chain rule.
- Applying the product rule as $(fg)'=f'g'$.
- Substituting numerical values before differentiating a related-rates equation.
- Confusing derivative notation with division, such as cancelling `dy/dx` without justification.
