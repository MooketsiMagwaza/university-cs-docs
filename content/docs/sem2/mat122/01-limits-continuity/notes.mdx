## Limits and Continuity

The limit $\lim_{x\to a}f(x)=L$ means that values of $f(x)$ approach $L$ as $x$ approaches $a$, regardless of whether $f(a)$ is defined or has that value. A two-sided limit exists only when the left-hand and right-hand limits agree.

## Evaluating limits

Direct substitution works for functions continuous at $a$. If substitution gives $0/0$, simplify: factor and cancel a common factor, rationalise a radical, or combine fractions. Cancellation may reveal a removable hole, but it does not redefine the original function at the excluded point.

For a piecewise function, calculate the left and right limits separately and compare them with the assigned value. The function is continuous at $a$ when $f(a)$ exists, the limit exists, and the limit equals $f(a)$.

## Infinite behaviour

An infinite limit describes unbounded growth near a vertical asymptote. End behaviour as $x\to\pm\infty$ can often be found by comparing leading powers. For rational functions, the highest-degree terms determine the horizontal behaviour when numerator and denominator degrees are compared.

## Useful laws

When the component limits exist, limits preserve sums, products, constants, and quotients with a non-zero denominator. The squeeze theorem proves a limit by bounding a function between two functions with the same limiting value.

## Common mistakes

- Confusing the value $f(a)$ with the limit near $a$.
- Cancelling a factor and forgetting the original domain restriction.
- Checking only one side of a piecewise limit.
- Treating an infinite limit as an ordinary real number.
