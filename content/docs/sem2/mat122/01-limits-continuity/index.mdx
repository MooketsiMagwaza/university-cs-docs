---
title: "Limits and Continuity"
description: "The idea of a limit, one-sided and infinite limits, algebraic techniques for evaluating them, the squeeze theorem, and continuity."
---

A limit describes the value a function approaches as its input approaches a point, whatever the function does at the point itself. This topic covers one-sided limits, the algebraic techniques for resolving $0/0$, infinite limits and end behaviour, the limit laws and the squeeze theorem, and the test for continuity. Derivatives and integrals are both defined through limits, so this is the idea the rest of the course rests on.

<LearningObjectives items={[
  "Explain what a limit means, and why it does not depend on the value of the function at the point.",
  "Evaluate a limit by direct substitution, or by factoring and cancelling, rationalising or combining fractions when substitution gives 0/0.",
  "Decide whether a two-sided limit exists by comparing the left-hand and right-hand limits, including for piecewise functions.",
  "Interpret an infinite limit as a vertical asymptote, and find end behaviour by comparing leading powers.",
  "Use the limit laws and the squeeze theorem.",
  "Test continuity at a point: the function is defined there, the limit exists, and the two are equal.",
]} />
