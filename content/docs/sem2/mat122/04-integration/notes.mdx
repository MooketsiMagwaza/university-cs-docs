## Integration

An antiderivative of $f$ is a function $F$ with $F'=f$. The indefinite integral records all antiderivatives: $\int f(x)\,dx=F(x)+C$.

## Basic rules

For $n\ne-1$, $\int x^n dx=x^{n+1}/(n+1)+C$, while $\int 1/x\,dx=\ln|x|+C$. Linearity allows constants and sums to be integrated term by term. Always differentiate the result to check it.

The definite integral is a limit of signed areas. If $F'=f$, the fundamental theorem gives

$$\int_a^b f(x)\,dx=F(b)-F(a).$$

It also gives $\frac d{dx}\int_a^x f(t)\,dt=f(x)$ when $f$ is continuous.

## Substitution

For a composite expression, let $u=g(x)$ and replace $g'(x)dx$ with $du$. Change the bounds when using a definite integral, or substitute back before evaluating with the original variable. The method reverses the chain rule.

Integration by parts reverses the product rule: $\int u\,dv=uv-\int v\,du$. Choose $u$ to become simpler after differentiation and $dv$ to be easy to integrate.

## Common mistakes

- Omitting the constant $C$ in an indefinite integral.
- Forgetting absolute values in $\ln|x|$.
- Mixing transformed and original bounds.
- Confusing signed area with total geometric area.
