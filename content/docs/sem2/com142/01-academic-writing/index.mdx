---
title: "Academic Writing"
description: "Writing as controlled reasoning for a reader: purpose and audience, claims and evidence, academic voice and clear sentences."
---

Academic writing is controlled reasoning for a particular reader. It answers a question, makes its scope clear, supports claims with evidence and signals how ideas relate. This topic sets the standards the rest of the course builds on: knowing your purpose and audience, separating claims from evidence, qualifying claims honestly and writing clear sentences.

<LearningObjectives items={[
  "Identify the purpose, the assignment verb and the needs of the reader before drafting.",
  "Separate a claim from the evidence that supports it and from your interpretation of that evidence.",
  "Qualify a claim to match the strength of the evidence, for example by writing that results suggest rather than prove when a study is observational.",
  "Revise a sentence for academic voice, using concrete subjects and strong verbs and removing filler, vague references and unsupported generalisations.",
  "Check a draft against a checklist covering the question, the central claim, the support, the limitations and the structure.",
]} />
