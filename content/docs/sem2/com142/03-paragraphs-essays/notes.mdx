## Paragraphs and Essays

A paragraph is a unit of reasoning, not merely a block of sentences. It should have a controlling idea and enough development for the reader to see why that idea matters.

## Paragraph pattern

Begin with a topic sentence that makes a claim. Add evidence or an example, explain how it supports the claim, and link the point to the thesis or next paragraph. Use transitions to show addition, contrast, cause, qualification, or consequence; do not use them as decoration.

## Essay structure

An introduction narrows from context to the problem or question and ends with a thesis or roadmap. Body sections each advance a distinct part of the argument. A conclusion synthesises what the reasoning establishes, answers the question directly, and may state implications without introducing a new major claim.

## Coherence

Repeat key terms consistently, use pronouns with clear antecedents, and order material by logic rather than by the order in which sources were found. A comparison may be organised point-by-point or subject-by-subject; state the basis of comparison so the reader can follow it.

## Paragraph test

Cover the topic sentence and ask what the paragraph is about. Cover the explanation and ask whether the evidence still proves the claim. If a sentence cannot be connected to the controlling idea, move it, rewrite it, or remove it.
