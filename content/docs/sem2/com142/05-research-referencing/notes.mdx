## Research and Referencing

Research is a traceable process: define a question, search strategically, evaluate sources, extract relevant evidence, synthesise it, and document where it came from.

## Search and evaluate

Break the question into concepts and synonyms. Use the library catalogue and databases for scholarly material, then follow references and citation links to related work. Evaluate authority, method, currency, relevance, and purpose rather than relying on appearance or search ranking.

## Synthesis

Synthesis compares sources around an idea. Group sources by claim, agreement, disagreement, method, or limitation instead of writing one isolated summary per source. Use a matrix with columns for the research question, evidence, assumptions, limitations, and relevance to your thesis.

## Citation practice

An in-text citation identifies the source at the point where its idea or words are used. The reference list gives enough information to locate it. Follow the required style for author order, date, title, italics, page numbers, DOI, and URL. Keep a citation manager or source table accurate from the first note.

## Ethical use

Paraphrasing changes wording and structure but still requires attribution. Quotations require exact transcription and quotation marks. Cite data, images, code, models, and distinctive ideas as well as sentences. Never invent a reference or cite a source you have not checked.

## Final audit

Compare every in-text citation with the reference list, verify that links and page numbers work, and check that the evidence actually supports the sentence in which it appears.
