## Group Research and Presentation

A group project needs shared ownership of the outcome and explicit responsibility for the work. Agree on the question, deliverables, standards, milestones, communication channel, and process for resolving disagreement before work fragments.

## Roles and workflow

Useful roles include coordinator, researcher, evidence checker, editor, designer, and presenter, but roles should rotate or remain accountable to the whole group. Use a task board with an owner, definition of done, dependency, and due date. Store sources and drafts in one controlled location.

## Meetings

An effective agenda states the purpose, decisions needed, and preparation. Record decisions, action owners, and deadlines. Raise a risk early: “The source pack does not contain enough recent evidence for section two” is actionable; silence until the night before is not.

## Integrating the final product

The group should share a thesis, terminology, visual style, citation method, and quality checklist. One editor should inspect the complete document or slide deck, but each member remains responsible for checking their claims and sources.

## Presenting together

Plan handovers, rehearse the timing, and ensure every presenter understands the whole argument. Slides should have one purpose per slide, readable text, accessible contrast, and cited media. Prepare for likely questions and agree how to respond when the answer is uncertain.

## Peer accountability

Give feedback on observable work and impact, not personality. If a contribution is late, restate the agreed requirement, offer a specific recovery task, and document the change. Escalate persistent non-participation through the course process with evidence of attempts to resolve it.
